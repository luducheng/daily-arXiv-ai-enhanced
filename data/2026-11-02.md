### [1] [Likely Metal Scarring in the Magnetic White Dwarf LHS 2534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40741)
*Ryan J. Souchet,Adam Moss,Jamie Tayar,Warren R. Brown,Mukremin Kilic,J. J. Hermes*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Time-resolved MMT spectroscopy of the magnetic white dwarf LHS 2534 reveals periodic variability in Na I and Cr I features, confirming magnetically guided accretion onto a rotating polar cap.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Only three white dwarfs are known to have metal-enriched polar caps from magnetically guided accretion, limiting study of planetesimal accretion and magnetic-field-driven atmospheric spreading; more targets are needed.

Method: An observing campaign on the magnetic white dwarf LHS 2534 using time-resolved spectroscopy at the MMT, tracking variability in the strength of Na I and Cr I features and comparing derived periods to K2 photometry.

Result: Na I and Cr I feature strengths vary with periods of 19.46 h and 19.36 h respectively, closely matching the 20.22 h period from K2 photometry.

Conclusion: Such variable objects can be detected and characterized spectroscopically; future time-series observations of similar targets will clarify how these metal-enriched atmospheres form.

Abstract: There are only three white dwarfs known to possess metal-enriched polar caps due to magnetically guided accretion. These rare systems offer a unique opportunity to study planetesimal accretion and how magnetic fields influence the spreading of material across the atmosphere. To better study these processes however, more targets are needed. Here we report our findings from an observing campaign on the magnetic white dwarf LHS 2534. Time-resolved spectra at the MMT show clear variability in the strength of the Na I and Cr I features. We find that the strengths of these features vary over a period of 19.46 and 19.36 hours respectively, closely matching the 20.22 hour period determined from K2 photometry. Our findings demonstrate the ability to detect and study these variable objects with spectroscopy, and time-series observations of similar targets in the future will provide more insight into how these atmospheres form.

### [2] [Spectroscopic observations of periodic bursty ejections during a dome-spire-shaped jet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41343)

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The formation and evolution of dome-spire (anemone) jets and their blob-like substructure are not quantitatively characterized in terms of multi-temperature plasma dynamics and 3D temporal evolution, limiting understanding of how null-point magnetic reconnection drives impulsive energy release and mass ejection.

Method: Unique sit-and-stare IRIS spectroscopy (Si IV 1402.77 Å, Mg II k, C II) plus slit-jaw imaging at 1400 Å and 2796 Å, with the slit placed just above the presumed reconnection site; complementary GST/FISS chromospheric H-alpha and Ca II 8542.10 Å spectra; EUV imaging from SDO/AIA for the hot component. Analysis includes multi-Gaussian decomposition of Si IV profiles for Doppler-component/acceleration retrieval.

Result: Jet formed by multiple periodic (~30 s) plasma ejections with blob-like appearance, each linked to strongly blueshifted Si IV transition-region emission reaching ~-300 km/s; ejections organized into well-defined 3–5 min packets. Only a single Doppler component appears in chromospheric Ca II. Multi-Gaussian fitting reveals a highly accelerated component with LOS speed rising linearly from ~30 to 112 km/s over 66 s (~1.3 km/s^2 acceleration). Kinetic energy release rate estimated at ~1.0e24–1.0e25 erg/s.

Conclusion: The observations indicate intermittent, periodic reconnection-driven ejections building a dome-spire jet, with a strongly accelerated transition-region plasma and substantial kinetic energy flux; the multi-temperature, multi-instrument approach quantitatively constrains jet dynamics and supports null-point reconnection as the driver, though the abstract does not explicitly state final interpretive conclusions.

Abstract: Context: The study investigates the complexity of the formation and evolution of a dome-spire-shaped jet (or anemone jet) that originated in an active region and was presumably driven by null-point magnetic reconnection.
  Aims: We aim to provide a quantitative estimation of multi-temperature plasma dynamics and physical parameters of the jet and its blob-like features, together with its temporal evolution in three-dimensional perspective.
  Methods: For this, we analyze unique sit-and-stare spectral (Si IV, Mg II k, and C II) and slit-jaw imaging 1400 Å and 2796 Å observations obtained with the Interface Region Imaging Spectrograph (IRIS), with a slit uniquely positioned just above the assumed reconnection site. More importantly, the ejections are grouped into well-defined packets, each lasting approximately 3--5 min. In contrast, only a single Doppler component is observed in the chromospheric Ca II line. Multi-Gaussian decomposition of the Si IV line profiles reveals a highly accelerated plasma, with line-of-sight speed toward the observer increasing linearly from $\sim$30 to 112 km/s over 66 s, corresponding to an acceleration of 1.3 km/s$^2$. The kinetic energy release rate is estimated in the range of $\sim$1.0$\times10^{24}$--1.0$\times10^{25}$ erg\,s$^{-1}$.

### [3] [Relative Influence of Coronal Properties of CME in their Evolution and Geo-effectiveness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41376)
*Jithu J Athalathil,Asif M Mandayapuram,Prateek Mayank,Bhargav Vaidya*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: A 400-member ensemble of global MHD simulations with SWASTi quantifies how CME density, speed, magnetic flux, and temperature prescribed at 0.1 AU affect heliospheric propagation and geoeffectiveness at Earth.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: CME near-Sun property uncertainties hamper space weather forecasting, so the sensitivity of CME evolution to initial parameters must be understood to improve predictions.

Primary parameters explain only ~half the output variance. Density shows the broadest influence. Mean arrival-time sensitivities: 2.43 h per 100 km/s in injection speed and 1.99 h per 10^-18 kg/m^3 in injection density. CME volume follows two-phase evolution: gradual expansion then power-law regime with median expansion index 2.38.

Conclusion: Parameter-specific priorities for CME forecasting are established: constrain speed for arrival-time prediction, density for duration and pressure-driven effects, and magnetic flux for geomagnetic response at Earth.

Abstract: Uncertainties in the coronal mass ejection (CME) properties near the Sun remain a major challenge for accurate space weather forecasting. Understanding the sensitivity of CME evolution to the initial parameters is therefore essential for improving the predictions. We investigate the influence of physical CME properties prescribed at 0.1 AU on their subsequent heliospheric propagation and geoeffectiveness at Earth, using the SWASTi framework. We perform a systematically sampled ensemble of 400 global magnetohydrodynamic simulations by varying the initial CME density, speed, magnetic flux and temperature. We quantify the relative influence of these parameters on CME evolution, plasma properties at L1, and the resulting geomagnetic response. The analysis reveals a clear hierarchy of influence: initial speed primarily controls the peak radial velocity and arrival time at L1, magnetic flux controls the magnetic-field strength, and density governs the peak density and magnetopause compression. Primary parameters account for only half the output variance, highlighting the collective influence of other parameters. Density exhibits the broadest influence across the analyzed outputs. We find a mean arrival-time sensitivities of 2.43h per 100 km/s variation in injection speed and 1.99h per $10^{-18}$ kg/m$^3$ variation in injection density. The CME volume exhibits a two-phase evolution, characterized by an initial gradual expansion followed by a power-law regime with a median expansion index of 2.38. These results establish parameter-specific priorities for CME forecasting, highlighting the importance of constraining CME speed for arrival-time prediction, density for duration and pressure-driven effects, and magnetic flux for geomagnetic response at Earth.

### [5] [Inferring dominant acceleration mechanisms using the longitudinal spread of solar particle intensities: 28 May 2021 event](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41749)

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This study disentangles SEP acceleration sources by tracking the longitudinal evolution of the 28 May 2021 event: 0.1 MeV electrons stay static while 14 and 32 MeV protons grow and drift westward, suggesting flare dominance for low-energy electrons and CME-shock dominance for protons, with interplanetary transport as a possible contributor.

Method: Characterize the longitudinal spread of the 28 May 2021 SEP event by fitting Gaussian curves at regular time intervals to particle intensities as a function of spacecraft position across a new multi-spacecraft fleet, going beyond earlier peak-focused, three-observer studies. A 2D SEP transport model is used to test whether the evolving profiles could instead be explained by interplanetary effects.

Abstract: Solar energetic particles (SEPs) are typically accelerated during solar eruptions and propagate along magnetic field lines in the inner heliosphere. These eruptions include shock waves driven by coronal mass ejections (CMEs) and magnetic reconnection in solar flares. Disentangling these source mechanisms within the measurements has been a long-standing problem for space physics researchers. Since spacecraft rarely reach the acceleration regions close to the Sun, measured SEP intensities reflect a combination of particle acceleration and interplanetary transport. The method used in this study attempts to identify which of the two main acceleration processes is more dominant for specific particle energies and species in a standalone event. Through this approach, we aim to better identify the signatures associated with these two acceleration phenomena and recognize similar behaviors in measurements with transport effects. To support the interpretation of the evolving profiles, we use a 2D SEP transport model to test if the evolution of the SEP distribution can also be caused by interplanetary effects. For this event, we find that the particle distribution of 0.1 MeV electrons remains static, while for 14 and 32 MeV protons, the distribution grows and moves to the west. Abstract: Turbulence properties in the solar wind change with increasing heliospheric distance.
