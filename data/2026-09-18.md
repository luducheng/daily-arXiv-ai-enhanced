### [1] [Effects of radial conductivity variation on the Ponomarenko dynamo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19187)
*Shipra Verma,Kannabiran Seshasayanan*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: The paper examines how radial conductivity variation affects the Ponomarenko dynamo, showing that the critical magnetic Reynolds number depends strongly on the conductivity profile near the velocity discontinuity, and that far from threshold the growth rate is governed by the local conductivity gradient at the discontinuity.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The Ponomarenko dynamo is a canonical kinematic dynamo model, but the role of spatially varying conductivity—relevant to realistic astrophysical and laboratory plasmas—has not been systematically characterized. The authors aim to determine how different radial conductivity profiles modify the dynamo threshold and growth rate, particularly near the dominant shear region at the velocity discontinuity.

Method: Kinematic dynamo calculations are performed with monotonically increasing/decreasing and sinusoidally varying radial conductivity profiles. The critical magnetic Reynolds number Rm_c and growth rate are computed in various limits, including sharp variation, large variations/wavenumbers, and large Rm (≫1). Comparisons are also made with realistic smooth velocity profiles and different magnetic boundary conditions.

Result: Rm_c strongly depends on the form of conductivity variation near the velocity discontinuity. For monotonically varying profiles in the sharp-variation limit, the threshold maps to the dynamo problem with a conductivity jump. For sinusoidal profiles with large variations/wavenumbers, the threshold increases due to effective diffusivity from rapid conductivity changes. Far from threshold, the growth rate depends only on the local conductivity gradient near the discontinuity; opposite signs of shear and conductivity gradient yield larger growth rates, aiding regeneration of B_φ from B_r. At large Rm, the conductivity variation modifies the growth rate by a term proportional to the local conductivity gradient times Rm^{-1/2}, depending only on the local gradient at the discontinuity.

Conclusion: Conductivity variation significantly influences the Ponomarenko dynamo. The dynamo threshold is controlled by the conductivity structure near the velocity discontinuity, while the growth rate away from threshold is determined solely by the local conductivity gradient there. The large-Rm correction scales as Rm^{-1/2} and is local in character. These dependencies persist for realistic smooth velocity profiles and different magnetic boundary conditions.

Abstract: We study the effects of conductivity variation on the Ponomarenko dynamo. Taking monotonically increasing/decreasing and sinusoidally varying radial profiles, we study the kinematic dynamo problem. The threshold of the dynamo, given by the critical magnetic Reynolds number Rm$_c$, is found to strongly depend on the form of conductivity variation near the discontinuity of the velocity field where the shear is dominant. For monotonically varying profiles in the limit of sharp variation, this threshold maps to the problem of the dynamo with a jump in conductivity, while for the sinusoidal profile with large variations/wavenumbers, the threshold increases due to effective diffusivity arising from the rapid changes in conductivity. Far from the threshold, it is found that the growth rate depends only on the local gradient of the conductivity near the discontinuity, with the opposite sign of shear and conductivity gradient leading to a larger growth rate, thereby aiding the dynamo instability in regenerating B$_φ$ from B$_r$. We calculate the expression for the growth rate in the limit of large Rm$(\gg 1)$ and find that the conductivity variation leads to a modification proportional to the local gradient of conductivity times Rm$^{-1/2}$. This correction is found to depend only on the local gradient of the conductivity at the discontinuity. Similar dependencies are found for realistic, smooth velocity profiles and for different magnetic boundary conditions.
