### [2] [Magnetic vector potentials and helicity in periodic domains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26901)
*Simon Candelaresi*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Magnetic vector potentials and magnetic helicity are analyzed in periodic (torus-like) and non-periodic domains; a vector potential exists only under specific flux conditions, and mean magnetic flux makes the standard magnetic helicity ill-defined/uninterpretable except via the winding gauge, where it recovers the geometric field-line-winding interpretation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Periodic domains and mean magnetic flux complicate the existence of a magnetic vector potential and the definition/interpretation of magnetic helicity, which has implications for solar and plasma physics.

Method: Physical arguments representing periodic domains as tori; differential-form representation of magnetic fields (2-forms) and vector potentials (1-forms) to determine existence conditions by periodicity class; thought experiments to test the geometric (field-line-linking) interpretation; helicity computation with gauge freedom analysis and use of the winding gauge.

Result: Singly periodic domains always admit a vector potential; doubly periodic require vanishing net flux through the non-periodic dimension; triply periodic require vanishing net flux in all directions. Periodic domains with a mean flux yield contradictory helicity values, and some fields have gauge-dependent, non-meaningful helicity. The winding gauge restores the geometric interpretation as flux-averaged field-line winding. For a non-periodic solar-relevant domain with zero integrated flux, an appropriate gauge transformation makes helicity vanish, while winding helicity does not.

Conclusion: With a mean magnetic field, the calculated magnetic helicity cannot be interpreted geometrically in a straightforward way; only the winding gauge recovers the field-line-winding interpretation, and gauge choice critically determines helicity values in periodic and non-periodic domains.

Abstract: For a magnetic field in three-dimensional Euclidean space, we argue about the existence of a magnetic vector potential in a finite cuboid with periodic boundary conditions. We first argue in physical terms by representing periodic domains as tori and show that in some cases, the generating electric current is unphysical. We then represent magnetic fields and vector potentials as differential 2 and 1-forms. For a given magnetic field we ask the question whether a corresponding vector potential exists. Singly periodic domains are shown to always allow for a vector potential. For doubly periodic domains it only exists if the net magnetic flux through the non-periodic dimension vanishes, while for triply periodic domains the net flux must vanish in all directions. This has implications for the definition of magnetic helicity in periodic domains. We show that periodic domains together with a mean magnetic flux lead to contradicting results about the value of the magnetic helicity and we show in a few thought experiments fundamental contradictions to the interpretation as the linking of magnetic field lines. For the helicity we can even construct magnetic fields in periodic domains for which the magnetic helicity exists, but the calculations of the magnetic helicity do not lead to meaningful results due to the gauge freedom. With that we conclude that in presence of a mean magnetic field the calculated magnetic helicity cannot be interpreted geometrically in a straightforward way. However, using the winding gauge we can recover the geometric interpretation of the helicity as the flux averaged magnetic field line winding. Finally, for the solar relevant non-periodic domain with finite point-wise flux and zero integrated flux, using an appropriate gauge transformation the magnetic helicity can be shown to vanish. The winding helicity, however, would lead to a non-vanishing value here.

Abstract: Buoyancy glitches in red giants provide localized information on the interior structure near the base of the convective envelope, but their properties are typically inferred from dedicated seismic modelling rather than global properties, limiting large-sample applications. We investigate whether the buoyancy-glitch amplitude ($A_g$), location ($W_{\rm g}^{\star}$), and width ($D_g$) can be recovered from global stellar properties using gradient-boosted tree regressors (XGBoost and LightGBM). The models were trained on a grid of 12580 synthetic stellar models of low-luminosity red giants, with target parameters derived from fits to the local buoyancy frequency profile. We find that $W_{\rm g}^{\star}$ and $D_g$ exhibit excellent recoverability, achieving test-set $\mathcal{R}^2$ values of 0.98 and 0.97, respectively. In contrast, $A_g$ shows moderate recoverability overall ($\mathcal{R}^2=0.68$). This reduced recoverability of $A_g$ is concentrated in the large frequency separation range $Δν= 4.6$--$18.9\,μ$Hz, where the glitch resides in the evanescent-zone transition between the convective envelope and the gravity-mode cavity, increasing intrinsic scatter. SHAP analysis shows that $ν_{\rm max}$ and $T_{\rm eff}$ dominate the predictions across all targets, indicating that the regressors rely primarily on evolutionary state. While $W_{\rm g}^{\star}$ and $D_g$ are tightly constrained by global evolutionary trends, the lower recoverability of $A_g$ reflects localized structural complexity associated with red-giant evolution. Overall, for the synthetic grid adopted here, these results indicate that global properties provide a direct estimate of the buoyancy-glitch parameters and a way to prioritise dedicated mixed-mode inversions for large asteroseismic samples such as Kepler and PLATO.
