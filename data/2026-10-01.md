### [1] [Surveying for Close Binaries among Lithium-rich Giants](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38320)
*Jesús Torres,Sebastián Vilaza-Dallago,Julio Chanamé,Rafael Brahm*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Multi-epoch radial velocities of 57 Li-rich giants show no enhanced close-binary fraction relative to Li-normal giants, challenging the binary-interaction enrichment scenario for anomalous Li.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: A leading scenario for anomalously high Li abundances in low-mass red giants invokes interaction with a close binary companion. If this channel dominates, Li-rich giants should exhibit a significantly higher binary fraction than Li-normal ones; testing this prediction constrains the enrichment mechanism.

Method: The authors monitored 70 Li-rich giants (40 red clump, 30 first-ascent RGB) with ESPRESSO at the VLT. The analysis uses 57 targets (32 RC + 25 RGB) with ≥4 visits over a ~7-month baseline. For stars showing RV variation exceeding intrinsic jitter expectations, a Monte Carlo analysis estimates the probability of a stellar (P_SB) or sub-stellar (P_SS) companion. The close binary fraction is then compared between RC and RGB sub-samples.

Result: Discounting 38 targets consistent with stellar jitter, 11 RC and 8 RGB stars (34% and 32% of their sub-samples) show RV variability consistent with a close companion (orbital period <3000 days); 3 of these 19 are confirmed stellar-mass companions. The P_SB and P_SS distributions are statistically indistinguishable between RC and RGB samples.

Conclusion: No evidence is found for a close-binary fraction among Li-rich giants, at either evolutionary stage, exceeding that expected for a Li-normal population, disfavoring the binary-interaction channel as the dominant Li-enrichment mechanism. Extending the RV baseline to probe longer orbital periods is needed to further test the scenario.

Abstract: One of the possible scenarios usually considered to explain the existence of low-mass red giants with anomalously high lithium (Li) abundances requires the interaction with a close binary companion. If dominant, this enrichment channel predicts a significantly enhanced binary fraction among Li-rich giants relative to Li-normal ones. We aim to constrain the binary interaction hypothesis by using multi-epoch radial velocities (RVs) to measure the close binary fraction of a statistically robust sample of Li-rich giants. We are surveying both red clump (RC) and first ascent red giant (RGB) targets, which allows us to examine potential differences in the binary fraction as a function of evolutionary stage. We are monitoring 70 targets (40 RC and 30 RGB, all Li-rich) using ESPRESSO at the VLT, 57 of which (32 RC + 25 RGB) already have 4 or more visits over a $\sim$7 month baseline; the analysis in this paper is based on the latter. For the targets with RV variation larger than that expected for intrinsic jitter, we use a Monte Carlo analysis to estimate the probability that the observed RV variation is due to a stellar ($P_{SB}$) or sub-stellar ($P_{SS}$) companion. Discounting the 38 targets consistent with stellar jitter, we are left with 11 RC and 8 RGB stars (34% and 32% of the respective sub-samples) with RV variability consistent with the presence of a close companion with an orbital period shorter than 3000 days. Of these 19, 3 can be confirmed as stellar-mass companions. The distributions of $P_{SB}$ and $P_{SS}$ are statistically indistinguishable between the RC and RGB samples. We thus find no evidence so far in our current data for a binary fraction for Li-rich giants (in either evolutionary stage) larger than that expected for a Li-normal population. Extending the RV baseline to probe longer orbital periods will be important to keep exploring the plausibility of this scenario.

### [2] [Magnetic field diagnostics of a solar active region filament](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38774)
*Daiki Yamasaki,Yu Wei Huang,Yuki Hashimoto,Satoru UeNo,Kiyoshi Ichimoto*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Spectropolarimetry of an active region filament in He I 10830 Å and Si I 10827 Å yields a ~100 G horizontal field aligned with the filament axis, plus anomalous Zeeman-like linear polarization signatures (~500 G) that arise in optically thick regions and expose limitations of current inversion frameworks.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To investigate the magnetic field structure of an active region filament and to clarify the classical normal- vs. reverse-polarity model distinction and possible photospheric contamination of the He I 10830 Å signal.

Method: Full-Stokes inversions with the HAZEL code (accounting for Zeeman and Hanle effects) applied to spectropolarimetric observations of a filament in He I 10830 Å and Si I 10827 Å; complementary inversions assuming only the Zeeman effect for anomalous profiles; comparison with simultaneous Si I 10827 Å photospheric field estimates.

Result: A mean field strength of 101 ± 33 G with a nearly axis-parallel horizontal field, making the normal/reverse-polarity distinction physically insignificant. Zeeman-like double-peaked symmetric linear polarization profiles appear in some pixels, implying ~500 G under Zeeman-only inversion, inconsistent with the simultaneous Si I 10827 Å photospheric field of <100 G, ruling out photospheric contamination. These profiles concentrate in optically thick regions (τ ~ 1.4-2.5).

Conclusion: The anomalous Zeeman-like profiles likely reflect breakdown of HAZEL's simplifying assumptions in optically thick regions rather than a genuine ~500 G field, motivating future radiative-transfer modeling with self-consistent radiation fields, differential illumination of multiplet components, and possibly partial frequency redistribution.

Abstract: We performed spectropolarimetric observations of an active region filament in He I 10830 angstrom and Si I 10827 angstrom lines to investigate its magnetic field structure. We carried out full-Stokes inversions with the HAZEL code, which takes into account the Zeeman and Hanle effects. As a result, we yielded a mean field strength of 101 $\pm$ 33 G and a horizontal field nearly parallel to the filament axis, such that the distinction between the two classical normal- and reverse-polarity models becomes physically insignificant. In addition, we found Zeeman-like signatures in the linear polarization, characterized by double-peaked symmetric profiles, in some pixels of our observations. Since these profiles could not be well reproduced by modeling that included both the Zeeman and Hanle effects, we performed inversions assuming only the Zeeman effect. The inversion yielded a strong magnetic field of approximately 500 G. However, simultaneous observations of Si I 10827 angstrom indicate a photospheric magnetic field weaker than 100 G. Therefore, the scenario proposed by Diaz Baso et al. (2016), in which the strong field inferred from He I 10830 angstrom originates from contamination by the underlying photosphere, does not apply to our filament. The Zeeman-like profiles are preferentially found in optically thick regions ($τ$ ~ 1.4-2.5), where the simplifying assumptions adopted in HAZEL are expected to become less reliable. Our results suggest that these profiles reveal limitations of the current inversion framework in optically thick regions and motivate future radiative-transfer modeling incorporating self-consistent radiation fields, differential illumination of the multiplet components, and possibly partial frequency redistribution.

### [3] [Towards breaking the degeneracy between stellar ages and unresolved multiplicity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39907)
*Friedrich Anders,Anna B. A. Queiroz,Sagar Malhotra,Cristina Chiappini,Arman Khalatyan*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper proposes using simulation-based inference (SBI) to infer stellar ages, masses, binary mass ratios, and other parameters from spectroscopic, photometric, and astrometric data, addressing the critical shortcoming of standard parameter inference codes that ignore unresolved stellar multiplicity.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Stellar age is a key parameter for disentangling Galactic formation scenarios, yet no age-dating method is fully model-independent and current estimates achieve only ~7% precision and ~10–15% accuracy. State-of-the-art codes like StarHorse fail to account for unresolved stellar multiplicity, which is essential—especially for nearly equal-mass binaries and higher-order main-sequence systems—for the next generation of stellar surveys.

Method: The authors outline how simulation-based inference (SBI), a robust and flexible approach suited to complex models where traditional likelihood-based methods become infeasible, can be applied to infer stellar parameters from combined spectroscopic, photometric, and astrometric measurements.

Result: SBI is sketched as a means to obtain precise stellar ages, masses, binary mass ratios, and other stellar parameters, with the proposed approach explicitly incorporating unresolved stellar multiplicity that conventional inference codes omit.

Conclusion: Properly accounting for unresolved multiplicity via SBI is presented as imperative for next-generation stellar surveys and offers a promising path toward more precise and accurate stellar parameter and age estimation.

Abstract: Stellar age is the central desirable parameter we need to disentangle different Galactic formation scenarios. Nevertheless, no stellar age dating method is completely model-independent. Even in the best-case scenarios, present-day stellar age estimates are only precise to $\sim7\%$ and accurate to $\sim10-15\%$. A main drawback of state-of-the-art stellar parameter inference codes like {\tt StarHorse} is that they do not take into account unresolved stellar multiplicity. Especially in the case of nearly equal-mass binaries or higher-order systems on the main sequence, properly taking into account unresolved multiplicity is imperative for the next generation of stellar surveys. Simulation-based inference (SBI) is a robust and flexible approach for dealing with complex models where traditional likelihood-based methods become unfeasible. Here we sketch how SBI can be used to obtain precise stellar ages, masses, binary mass ratios, and other stellar parameters from spectroscopic, photometric, and astrometric measurements.

### [4] [Tracing star formation across the Rosette nebula using deep Spitzer imaging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40021)
*Daniel Capela,Aleks Scholz,Koraljka Muzic,Herve Bouy,Belinda Damian,Victor Almendros-Abad,Amelia Bayo*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: A deep Spitzer/IRAC survey of the Rosette Nebula catalogued ~20,000 sources and identified 1,528 YSOs (1,303 Class II, 225 Class I), revealing that star formation has ceased in the central clusters but is ongoing in an annulus at 11-16 pc, consistent with triggering by the expanding HII region.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The study aims to characterize the young stellar population of the Rosette Nebula and to test whether the expanding HII region is triggering sequential star formation, which requires a large, uniformly selected YSO census across the nebula.

Method: Stacked archival Spitzer/IRAC images were processed with the DeNeb software to remove nebulous emission. A catalogue of ~20,000 sources with photometry in all four IRAC bands (3.6-8.0 microns) was compiled and complemented with near-infrared and optical data. YSOs were identified via multi-filter colour-colour criteria, yielding 1,528 YSOs (1,303 Class II, 225 Class I), which were then grouped into clusters and used to derive Class I ratios and disk fractions.

Result: 1528 YSOs were selected, including three newly identified groups alongside previously known clusters. The main central clusters NGC 2244 and NGC 2237 are almost devoid of Class I sources, whereas in an annulus at radii of 11-16 pc, roughly coinciding with the expanding HII front, about 30% of YSOs are Class I. Groups further out also show high Class I ratios, and similar patterns appear in disk fractions.

Conclusion: The observed spatial distribution of Class I sources and disk fractions is consistent with star formation triggered by the expanding HII region, combined with ongoing star formation beyond the ionisation front.

Abstract: We present a deep large-scale infrared survey of young stellar objects in the Rosette Nebula. Our analysis is based on stacked archival Spitzer/IRAC images. The images have been treated with the software DeNeb to remove nebulous emission. Our catalogue contains ~20000 sources with photometry in all 4 IRAC bands from 3.6 to 8.0 microns, complemented with near-infrared and optical data. From this database, we identify YSOs using multi-filter colour-colour criteria. We select 1528 YSOs, from which 1303 are Class II and 225 are Class I. Using this sample, we compile a list of clusters and groups of YSOs in the Rosette region, including previously known ones, plus three newly identified groups. Using our census of YSOs, in combination with the literature, we derive the Class I ratios and disk fractions for clusters in the nebula. The main clusters in the central cavity, NGC 2244 and NGC 2237 are almost devoid of Class I sources, indicating that star formation has ceased in this area. On the other hand, in an annulus around the centre with radii of 11-16 pc, roughly coinciding with the expanding HII front, about 30% of the YSOs are Class I, a clear sign of ongoing star formation. Other groups further out in the cloud also exhibit a high Class I ratio. Similar patterns are seen in the disk fractions. Our results are consistent with possible triggering by the expanding HII region, combined with star formation beyond the ionisation front.

### [5] [MAGiDiff: Sampling the Photospheric Vector Field from UV/EUV Filtergrams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40043)
*Ruoyu Wang,David Fouhey*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: MAGiDiff, a denoising diffusion model, estimates disambiguated photospheric vector magnetograms from SDO/AIA UV/EUV filtergrams, mimicking Hinode/SOT-SP ground truth and generalizing across solar cycles and instruments.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Photospheric vector magnetic fields are foundational to modeling, understanding, and forecasting solar activity, but are usually produced by inverting and disambiguating the full Stokes vector at multiple passbands, which is demanding. The authors investigate whether vector magnetograms can instead be estimated from UV/EUV filtergrams, a challenging and intrinsically ambiguous problem due to the indirect, ill-posed mapping from UV/EUV intensity to magnetic field without polarization information.

Method: MAGiDiff is a machine-learning method based on denoising diffusion models. It takes as input a stack of filtergrams from SDO/AIA and is trained to output the disambiguated vector magnetogram as seen by Hinode/SOT-SP.

Result: MAGiDiff accurately mimics the Hinode ground truth. It reproduces physical structure and magnetic connectivity, produces plausible structures for active regions on full-disk, generalizes across solar cycles despite hemispheric polarity reversal, and can be fine-tuned to other EUV instruments including STEREO/EUVI and GOES-R/SUVI.

Conclusion: While MAGiDiff is clearly not a substitute for a dedicated instrument, it opens the door to new capabilities for estimating photospheric vector magnetograms without polarization measurements.

Abstract: Photospheric vector magnetic fields are foundational to modeling, understanding, and forecasting solar activity. These data are usually produced by inverting and disambiguating the full Stokes vector at multiple passbands, which is demanding. Here, we investigate how well we can estimate photospheric vector magnetograms from UV/EUV filtergrams. This problem is challenging and intrinsically ambiguous without polarization information, as the mapping from UV/EUV intensity to the magnetic field is indirect and ill-posed. We introduce MAGiDiff, a machine-learning-based method that uses denoising diffusion models to estimate vector magnetograms from UV/EUV filtergrams. As input, MAGiDiff takes a stack of filtergrams from the Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO) / Atmospheric Imaging Assembly (AIA); as output, it is trained to estimate the disambiguated vector magnetogram as seen by Hinode / Solar Optical Telescope-Spectro-Polarimeter (SOT-SP). We show that MAGiDiff can accurately mimic the Hinode ground-truth. Additionally, we probe MAGiDiff's understanding of the physical structure and magnetic connectivity. On full-disk, we show that it produces plausible structures for active regions. MAGiDiff generalizes across solar cycles despite hemispheric polarity reversal, and can be fine-tuned to other EUV instruments including STEREO/EUVI and GOES-R/SUVI. While clearly not a substitute for a dedicated instrument, MAGiDiff opens the door to new capabilities.

</details>
