### [2] [Improvements to Asteroseismic Fitting of White Dwarfs in the Gaia Era](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04323)
*Andrew H. Dublin,Keaton J. Bell,Agnès Bischoff-Kim*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper presents improved statistical methodologies for white dwarf asteroseismology, addressing challenges in constraining interior structures of pulsating white dwarfs through model grid-fitting. The authors demonstrate parameter recovery experiments showing that finer model grid interpolation, incorporation of Gaia astrometry absolute magnitude, mode identification consistency, chi-squared-based solution criteria, and Gaussian fitting to probability distributions all improve characterization of degenerate asteroseismic solutions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: White dwarf asteroseismology aims to constrain pulsating white dwarf interiors by fitting observed pulsation periods to stellar models, but the model grid-fitting approach faces obstacles such as degenerate asteroseismic solutions, sparse model grids missing solutions, and difficulty in robustly characterizing candidate solutions with uncertainties.

Method: The authors simulate parameter recovery experiments in one and two dimensions (mass and effective temperature) and employ several methodological improvements: (1) interpolating model periods onto finer grids, (2) incorporating absolute magnitude from Gaia astrometry into statistical fitting, (3) fitting to consistent model pulsation modes to isolate degenerate solutions, (4) adopting a chi-squared-based criterion for reasonable fits and identifying all matching model period combinations, and (5) fitting Gaussians to probability distributions for robust characterization of candidate solutions with uncertainties. Solutions are validated against direct likelihood marginalization.

Result: The proposed methods successfully resolve issues with degenerate asteroseismic solutions. Finer grid interpolation captures solutions missed by sparse grids, Gaia absolute magnitude reduces degeneracy, consistent mode fitting isolates individual candidate solutions, and Gaussian fitting to probability distributions provides robust characterization with uncertainties. The recovered solution landscape agrees well with direct likelihood marginalization.

Conclusion: The improved fitting methodologies enable accurate characterization of degenerate asteroseismic solution landscapes and can be generalized to higher-dimensional parameter spaces with more than two free parameters, offering a pathway to more reliable white dwarf interior structure constraints.

Abstract: White dwarf asteroseismology aims to constrain the interior structures of pulsating white dwarf stars by fitting observed pulsation periods to those computed for stellar models. However, there remain several obstacles for achieving reliable results with the model grid-fitting approach. We simulate data for a parameter recovery experiment in one and two dimensions (mass and effective temperature) to demonstrate improved methodologies that address challenges for characterizing degenerate asteroseismic solutions. We show that interpolating model periods onto a finer grid can adequately resolve asteroseismic solutions that would be missed with sparse model grids. Incorporating absolute magnitude from Gaia astrometry into the statistical fitting is shown to reduce solution degeneracy. We define a seismic solution as a solution to the mode identification problem, and we show that fitting to consistent model pulsation modes can isolate each candidate solution in degenerate solution space. A criterion based on $χ^2$ for what should be considered a reasonable fit is adopted, and we identify all combinations of model periods that meet this criterion, not only those nearest to the measured periods. Finally, we demonstrate how fitting Gaussians to probability distributions allows for the robust characterization of each candidate solution, including uncertainties. Our accurate characterization of the degenerate solution landscape is supported by the comparison of our solutions to a direct marginalization of the likelihood function. These fitting approaches can be generalized to higher dimensions, where there are more than two free parameters.

### [3] [Can isolated binaries form unequal-mass binary black-hole mergers with a high-spin primary black hole?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05128)
*Xiao-Tian Xu,Dong Lai,Bin Liu*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: The paper investigates whether isolated binary evolution can produce unequal-mass merging binary black holes (BBHs) with a high-spin primary BH, as an alternative to hierarchical mergers suggested by GWTC-5 and GW241110 observations.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: GWTC-5 observations reveal a subpopulation of merging BBHs with high-spin primary BHs and potentially unequal masses. GW241110 shows large spin-orbit misalignment, hinting at hierarchical merger origin, but other scenarios remain possible. The authors aim to test whether isolated binary evolution offers viable alternatives.

Method: Instead of full population synthesis, the authors analytically examine evolutionary pathways of forming merging BBHs, identifying possible routes to produce unequal-mass BBHs with high-spin primaries and assessing uncertainties for each pathway.

Result: Two pathways are identified: (1) mass-ratio reversal in initially wide binaries making the tidally spun-up second-born BH more massive and rapidly rotating than the first-born BH; (2) chemically homogeneous evolution of the primary star in an initially close unequal-mass binary, while the secondary evolves normally, producing a high-spin first-born BH more massive than its companion. Large spin-orbit misalignment can arise from large natal kicks or tertiary-induced precession/Zeipel-Lidov-Kozai oscillations.

Conclusion: Hierarchical mergers are not uniquely required to explain unequal-mass BBHs with high-spin primaries, though each isolated-binary pathway faces significant theoretical constraints. Future detection of more events with primary BH spins around ~0.7 would help discriminate between binary evolution and hierarchical merger origins.

Abstract: GWTC-5 has revealed a subpopulation of merging binary black holes (BBHs) with a high-spin primary black hole (BH) and possibly unequal BH masses. GW241110 additionally exhibits a large spin-orbit misalignment, which suggests a hierarchical-merger origin. However, other formation scenarios are possible or even likely, especially for events without constraints on spin-orbit misalignment. As an alternative to hierarchical mergers, we investigate whether binary evolution can produce unequal-mass BBH mergers with a high-spin primary BH. Rather than performing comprehensive population-synthesis calculations, we examine the evolutionary pathways of forming merging BBHs and assess their uncertainties. We identify two possible pathways for producing unequal-mass BBHs with a high-spin primary. In initially wide binaries, mass-ratio reversal can make the tidally spun-up second-born BH both more massive and more rapidly rotating than the first-born BH; alternatively, in an initially close, unequal-mass binary, the primary star may evolve chemically homogeneously, while the secondary star evolves normally, producing a high-spin first-born BH that is more massive than its companion. Generally, large spin-orbit misalignment can be produced by large natal kicks or tertiary-induced nodal precession and/or Zeipel-Lidov-Kozai oscillations. We conclude that hierarchical mergers are not uniquely required to produce unequal-mass BBHs with a high-spin primary BH, although each isolated-binary pathway faces important theoretical constraints. Future detections of more merger events with primary BH spins around 0.7 would discriminate between binary evolution and hierarchical merger origin.

### [4] [Energy Partitioning at the Termination Shock](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05267)
*Judit Szente,Bart van der Holst,Gabor Toth,Merav Opher*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper presents improvements to a global 3D MHD simulation of the outer heliosphere by introducing a new approach that distributes non-adiabatic shock heating among cold protons, electrons, and pickup ions (PUIs) while conserving total energy. The new methodology better reproduces Voyager 2 observations of the termination shock temperature jump (factor 10-20), showing that ~100% of non-adiabatic shock heating going to PUIs matches the observed jump conditions, which is key to understanding heliospheric physics and bridging kinetic simulation energy partitioning into global MHD models.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Previous simulations channeled all non-adiabatic shock heating to cold protons, producing excessively large temperature jumps at the termination shock relative to Voyager 2 measurements. The goal is to improve simulation fidelity and to understand the physical processes governing energy partitioning at the termination shock among different plasma populations.

Method: A novel energy partitioning scheme is implemented in the Boston University outer heliosphere MHD model that distributes non-adiabatic shock heating among cold protons, electrons, and pickup ions while conserving total energy of all ion populations and electrons. The model results are compared against Voyager 2 termination shock observations.

Result: The simulations reproduce the cold solar wind temperature jump of a factor 10-20 observed by Voyager 2 at the termination shock. Results show that allocating nearly 100% of non-adiabatic shock heating to PUIs reproduces the observed jump conditions along the V2 trajectory.

Conclusion: The work demonstrates how energy partitioning at shocks from kinetic simulations can be effectively incorporated into global MHD models, and confirms that PUIs dominate non-adiabatic heating at the termination shock, consistent with their significant role in shaping the heliosphere (e.g., deflating the inner heliosheath).

Abstract: We show new results of a global 3D magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulation of the Boston University outer heliosphere model where we used a newly developed approach that distributes the non-adiabatic shock heating among the cold protons, electrons, and pickup-ions (PUIs), while maintaining total energy conservation of all ions (cold protons and PUIs) and electrons. In our previous simulations ( E.S. Bair et al. 2025; B. van der Holst et al. 2026), all non-adiabatic shock heating was channeled to the cold protons, resulting in a too large temperature jump at the termination shock (TS) for the thermal solar wind. Using a new methodology we improved the simulation results with respect to the temperature jump observed at the TS by Voyager 2 (V2) spacecraft. Our simulations approached the observed jump conditions of a factor 10--20 in the cold solar wind temperature V2 measurements, and we obtain improvements in the simulation results relative to the data. Because we directly estimate in this way the distribution of non-adiabatic heating at the TS, we have the opportunity to study the physical process of heating cold plasma and PUIs in the TS. The results show that having almost 100\% non-adiabatic shock heating going towards PUIs at the TS reproduces the jump conditions observed along the V2 trajectory. This information is key to understanding the physical processes that shape the heliosphere. As shown by M. Opher et al. (2020), PUIs significantly change the shape of the heliosphere, for example, the presence of hot PUIs results in a deflated inner heliosheath. Our work provides the architecture of how energy partitioning at shocks in kinetic simulations (J. Giacalone et al. 2021) can be utilized in global MHD models.

### [5] [ROCKETS I: Investigating the Impact of the Rocket Effect on Two Nearby Young Open Clusters Encased in Infrared Bubbles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05272)

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: ROCKETS is a Gaia-based programme detecting kinematic signatures of stellar feedback ("rocket effect") in young star-forming regions. Analyzing two clusters (Collinder 69 and IC 1396), the authors find a strong excess of YSOs moving away from ionising O-type stars (RPMA <15°), quantified by a Rocket Effect Index (REI) of ~1.1–1.7 for all stars and >10 for Class I/II YSOs only. Pairwise expansion analysis and anisotropic position angles aligned with bright-rimmed clouds further support feedback-driven acceleration and triggered star formation.

Motivation: To demonstrate a new methodology capable of detecting kinematic fingerprints of stellar feedback ("rocket effect") in young star-forming regions using Gaia astrometry, and to show that the rocket effect drives gas and triggers star formation toward higher-density regions of expanding bubbles.

Method: Selects co-moving candidates via an astrometric ellipse defined by the median and MAD of Class I/II YSOs with Gaia data. Computes a relative proper-motion angle (RPMA) for each star relative to the ionising source. Introduces the Rocket Effect Index (REI) to quantify the excess of sources moving outward (RPMA <15°). Conducts pairwise velocity-structure/expansion analysis and examines Gaia colour-magnitude diagrams for age offsets. Analyzes position-angle anisotropy of outward-moving sources.

Result: Both regions show a strong excess of sources at RPMA <15° (moving away from ionising sources), with REI ≈ 1.1 (Collinder 69) and REI ≈ 1.7 (IC 1396), rising to REI ≥ 10 when only Class I/II YSOs are considered. The RPMA <15° subset exhibits stronger positive pairwise expansion than the full co-moving population. Outward-moving stars are anisotropically distributed, preferentially aligned toward bright-rimmed clouds. No significant age offset is found between outward-moving sources and literature cluster members.

Conclusion: The observed kinematic signatures support feedback-driven acceleration of gas via the rocket effect and triggered star formation preferentially directed toward higher-density regions of expanding bubbles around massive O-type stars.

Abstract: We introduce ROCKETS (Rocket-driven Cluster Kinematics & Triggered Star-formation), a Gaia based programme investigating kinematic fingerprints of stellar feedback or rocket effect in young star forming regions. We analyse two young clusters with O-type stars enclosed by mid-infrared bubbles, Collinder 69 and IC 1396 as a methodological demonstration on two nearby benchmark regions. Co-moving candidates are selected within an astrometric ellipse defined by the median and median absolute deviation of Class I/II YSOs with Gaia data. For each star, we compute a relative proper-motion angle (RPMA) relative to the ionising source. In both regions the RPMA distribution shows a strong excess at RPMA $<15^{\circ}$, corresponding to sources moving away from the respective ionising sources. Velocity-structure analysis of the sample shows that the RPMA $<15^\circ$ subset has stronger positive pairwise expansion than the full co-moving population, providing additional support for the rocket effect. The outward-moving stars are not uniformly distributed; instead, their position angles are strongly anisotropic and preferentially aligned towards the bright-rimmed clouds. Gaia colour-magnitude diagrams show no strong age offset between sources moving outward and literature cluster members. The observed kinematic signatures support feedback-driven acceleration of gas and triggered star formation preferentially towards higher density regions of expanding bubbles due to the rocket effect.

### [6] [Metallicity dependence of Wolf-Rayet binaries using detailed binary models: An absence of long-period systems at low metallicity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05398)
*Max M. Briel,Eirini Kasdagli,Tassos Fragos,Tomer Shenar,Jeff J. Andrews,Denzel Goh,Abhishek Chattaraj,Seth Gossage,Philipp Srivastava*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Using POSYDON binary evolution models across metallicities from 0.01 to 1 Z_sun, the paper shows that the absence of long-period WR binaries at low metallicity (SMC, LMC) is explained by Case-B mass transfer only partially stripping donors and weak WR winds at low Z, while Case-A mass transfer produces a short-period peak (~5-10 days) matching observations across metallicities.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Observations reveal a metallicity-dependent period distribution of WR binaries—short-period systems dominate in the SMC and LMC while the Milky Way extends to longer periods with some deficit compared to O-star progenitors—and the authors aim to characterize this population variation and determine the metallicity dependence of WR binary formation, especially regarding the period distribution.

Method: The authors use detailed binary evolution models from the POSYDON population synthesis code to predict WR binary populations at five metallicities (0.01, 0.1, 0.2, 0.45, and 1 Z_sun), analyzing the resulting period distributions and formation channels, then comparing them against observed WR binary populations in the SMC, LMC, and Milky Way.

Result: The absence of wide WR binaries at low metallicity is explained by Case-B mass transfer only partially stripping the donor while low-metallicity WR winds are too weak to remove the remaining envelope, eliminating the long-period peak present at solar metallicity. A short-period peak (~5-10 days) from Case-A mass transfer matches observations across all metallicities. Stable mass transfer and non-interacting systems dominate WR binary formation at all Z with no metallicity dependence in their relative contributions, implying SMT scales with metallicity identically to isolated WR formation. Common envelope evolution mainly produces short-period (<1 day) WR binaries with BH companions.

Conclusion: The metallicity dependence of the WR binary period distribution is driven by the interplay between Case-B mass transfer stripping efficiency and WR wind strength, yielding a physical explanation for observed differences between low-metallicity (SMC/LMC) and Galactic populations. Stable mass transfer is the key channel producing observationally matched short-period WR binaries independent of metallicity.

Abstract: Observations of Wolf-Rayet (WR) stars in binaries in the SMC and LMC suggest a preference for short period ($P\lesssim30$ days) orbits, with an apparent absence of WRs in long-period systems. The Galactic population does extend to longer periods but shows a deficit in long-period WR binaries compared to their progenitors, the O-star population. Across these populations a nearly constant binary fraction has been observed. We aim to characterize the population of WR binaries across a range of metallicity and determine the metallicity dependence of the formation of these systems, specifically focusing on the period distribution. We use detailed binary evolution models from POSYDON to predict the population of WR binaries at 0.01, 0.1, 0.2, 0.45, and $1Z_\odot$, analyzing the resulting period distribution and formation channels, while comparing them against the observed population in the SMC, LMC, and Milky Way. We find that the absence of wider WR binaries in the SMC, and potentially in the LMC, can be explained by stable, Case-B mass transfer only partially stripping the donor star, combined with WR winds at low metallicity being insufficiently strong to strip the remaining envelope. As a result, the long-period peak from Case-B mass transfer, present at Zsun, disappears at low metallicity. We additionally find that stable mass transfer (SMT) produces a short-period peak through Case-A mass transfer (P~5-10 days) that closely matches observations across metallicity. Furthermore, SMT and non-interacting systems are the dominant formation channels of WR binaries at all metallicities, with their relative contribution showing no metallicity dependence. This result implies that the SMT channel has the same metallicity-dependence as isolated WR formation. At the same time, we find that common envelope evolution primarily produces short-period ($P<1$ day) WR binaries with black-hole companions.
