*Edson Otoniel,Iarley P. Lobo*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper studies how modified electron dispersion relations affect white dwarf structure, finding that a logarithmic model can increase or decrease maximum mass depending on deformation parameter sign, while a simpler model yields only modest stiffening.

Motivation: To investigate whether modified electron dispersion relations (beyond standard special relativity) can significantly alter the structure and maximum mass of cold white dwarfs, potentially isolating the effects of modified electron kinematics on compact star structure.

Method: Modified electron dispersion relations are introduced into the degenerate electron equation of state via single-particle energy and group velocity, while stellar mass density is governed by ions via charge neutrality (ρ ≈ μ_e m_u n_e). The resulting EOS is coupled to unmodified TOV equations. Two models are tested: a baseline deformation and a logarithmic model.

Abstract: We investigate how modified electron dispersion relations affect the structure of cold white dwarfs (WDs). The deformation is introduced only in the degenerate electron equation of state, through the energy of a single particle and the group velocity, while the stellar mass density remains dominated by ions through the relation $ρ\simeq μ_e m_u n_e$ imposed by charge neutrality. The resulting equations of state are coupled to the standard TOV equations, with no modification of the gravitational field equations. For a baseline composed of carbon and oxygen with $μ_e=2$, the undeformed limit recovers a maximum mass in the Chandrasekhar scale, validating the normalization of the calculation before the modified cases are considered. The first model of modified dispersion produces only a modest stiffening over the parameter range studied, whereas the logarithmic model depends strongly on the sign of the deformation parameter: negative values increase the pressure and the maximum mass, while positive values soften the sequence.
