### [1] [SDSS-IV MaStar: Determination of Stellar Parameters Using Bayesian Averaging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13455)
*Yan-Ping Chen,Renbin Yan,Szabolcs Mészáros,Claudia Maraston,Daniel Thomas,Daniel Lazarz,Guy S. Stringfellow,Lewis Hill,Julie Imig,Joseph D. Gelfand,Jon A. Holtzman,Matthew Bershady,Dmitry Bizyaev,Niv Drory,Keivan G. Stassun*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Stellar parameters (Teff, log g, [M/H], [α/M]) are derived for 59,266 high-quality MaStar spectra via Bayesian full-spectrum fitting with BOSZ and MARCS models.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The MaStar library needs homogeneous, well-characterized stellar parameters to enable its use as a spectral reference and for stellar population studies.

Method: Gaia-based color and absolute magnitude priors select a model subset per star from BOSZ (ATLAS-9) and MARCS grids; full-spectrum fitting yields likelihoods, and likelihood-weighted mean parameters are adopted, with quality flags assigned.

Result: Parameters span Teff 2,600–29,861 K, log g 0–5.5, [M/H] −4.9 to 1.0, and [α/M] −0.98 to 1.0; median S/N per pixel is 96. Comparison with APOGEE and Gaia for common stars shows general consistency within uncertainties, though artifacts and systematic differences remain.

Conclusion: The catalog provides stellar parameters for 24,130 unique stars (59,266 spectra), publicly available via the MaStar SDSS-IV DR17 value-added catalog; systematic differences and artifacts are discussed.

Abstract: We present the stellar parameters for 59,266 high-quality spectra of 24,130 unique stars from the MaNGA Stellar Library (MaStar) in the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) Data Release 17 (DR17). The median signal-to noise ratio per pixel of the spectra is 96. We derive four stellar parameters, effective temperature (Teff), surface gravity (log g), metallicity ([M/H]), and {$α$}-enhancement ratio ($\rm [α/M]$), by comparing the data with BOSZ (ATLAS-9 based) and MARCS theoretical atmospheric models. We adopt a Bayesian method and use color and absolute magnitude derived from Gaia to select a subset of theoretical models for each star. We then perform full-spectrum fitting to estimate the likelihood of each model in the subset and then compute their likelihood-weighted mean parameters as the final parameters. We set stellar-parameter quality flags to facilitate the use of the derived stellar parameters. The MaStar stellar parameters derived herein span an effective temperature range of $\rm 2,600 \leq T_{eff} \leq 29,861 K$, a surface gravity range of $0 \leq \log g \leq 5.5$, a metallicity range of $-4.9 \leq \rm[M/H] \leq 1.0$, and an {$α$}-abundance range of $\rm -0.98 \leq [α/M] \leq 1.0$. We compare these parameters with those from APOGEE and Gaia for stars in common, finding general consistency within the uncertainties. However, some artifacts and systematic differences are present, and we discuss their potential causes. These new stellar parameters are available through the MaStar SDSS-IV DR17 value-added catalog (https://www.sdss4.org/dr17/mastar/mastar-stellar-parameters/).

### [2] [The JWST Proto-PAH Project: Detection of the methyl radical CH$_{3}$ in the highly evolved C-rich object SMP LMC 011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15905)
*Jialu Li,D. A. García-Hernández,A. Manchado,Debayan Das,J. Cami,Els Peeters,G. C. Sloan,B. Aringer,J. Bernard-Salas,C. Bhatt,Nicholas Clark,Harriet L. Dinerstein,M. A. Gómez-Muñoz,Kathleen E. Kraemer,M. Matsuura,R. Sahai,N. C. Sterling,Kevin Volk,G. M. Wahlgren,A. A. Zijlstra*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: First JWST MIRI/MRS detection of the methyl radical CH3 in an evolved C-rich object, SMP LMC 011, with Tex~190 K and Ntot~5.6e17 cm^-2, coupled with non-detection of C2H6, supporting a methyl-addition pathway toward alkyl-substituted aromatics.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The formation pathways of complex hydrocarbons and PAHs in C-rich circumstellar environments remain poorly understood, and the reactive intermediates driving aromatic growth have lacked direct observational constraints.

Method: JWST MIRI/MRS spectroscopy of the highly evolved C-rich object SMP LMC 011; excitation-temperature and column-density fitting of CH3; assessment of C2H6 non-detection; comparison with benzene abundance.

Result: CH3 detected with Tex ~190 K and Ntot ~5.6e17 cm^-2; C2H6 not detected, consistent with preferential CH3 reaction with unsaturated radicals over self-recombination; exceptionally large benzene column density; evidence for methyl-addition toward alkyl-substituted aromatics; CH3 attributed to UV- and/or shock-driven erosion of HAC dust in the dense warm torus.

Conclusion: CH3 is established as a key reactive intermediate in complex hydrocarbon formation in C-rich circumstellar environments; chemical models incorporating methyl-addition reactions are required to understand PAH formation in evolved stars; future detection of toluene and ethylbenzene would directly confirm CH3-driven aromatic growth.

Abstract: We report the first detection of the neutral methyl radical CH$_3$ in a highly evolved C-rich object, SMP LMC 011, using JWST MIRI/MRS. CH$_3$ is well fitted by an excitation temperature of $T_{\rm ex} \simeq$~190\,K and a column density of $N_{\rm tot}(\mathrm{CH_3}) \simeq 5.6\times10^{17}$ cm$^{-2}$. We also report the non-detection of ethane (C$_2$H$_6$), consistent with CH$_3$ reacting preferentially with unsaturated radicals rather than self-recombination, which may be inefficient in SMP LMC 011. Combined with the exceptionally large benzene column density of SMP LMC 011, this supports a methyl-addition route from benzene towards alkyl-substituted aromatics. We propose that the high CH$_3$ abundance is driven by the erosion of hydrogenated amorphous carbon (HAC) dust grains in the dense warm torus by UV photons from the central star and/or by shocks, which release CH$_3$ directly into the gas phase. These results establish CH$_3$ as a key reactive intermediate in the formation of complex hydrocarbons in C-rich circumstellar environments. Chemical models that incorporate methyl-addition reactions are needed for a better understanding of PAH formation pathways in evolved stars. The potential detection of alkyl-substituted aromatics such as toluene (C$_7$H$_8$) and ethylbenzene (C$_8$H$_{10}$) in C-rich evolved stars would provide direct confirmation of CH$_3$-driven aromatic growth in circumstellar environments.

</details>
