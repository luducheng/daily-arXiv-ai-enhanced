Main category: astro-ph.SR

Motivation: Magnetic fields have been proposed to suppress low-frequency gravity modes in stellar interiors, offering a way to constrain internal field strengths, but prior suppression criteria relied on linear theory and may be incomplete.

Method: Two-dimensional nonlinear magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations of a core-convecting star, examining gravity-wave interaction with magnetic fields of varying strength and geometry (both global and local field configurations).

Abstract: Magnetic fields have been proposed as a mechanism for suppressing low-frequency gravity modes in stellar interiors, providing a means of constraining internal magnetic field strengths. However, previous suppression criteria have largely relied on linear theory. Here, we use two-dimensional nonlinear magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations in a core convecting star to investigate the interaction of gravity waves with magnetic fields of varying strengths and geometries. Using both global and local field geometries, we find that amplitude reduction is gradual and frequency dependent, with low-frequency modes preferentially attenuated while higher-frequency modes can be amplified. In order to achieve significant suppression we require a magnetic field strength at least an order of magnitude larger than predicted by commonly used linear criteria. Its likely that this difference arises because magnetic fields can themselves excite waves through Maxwell stresses, offsetting magnetic attenuation. Our results demonstrate that strong magnetic modifications to wave propagation do not necessarily result in equivalent suppression of the global oscillation spectrum. The linear criteria typically used to infer field strengths based on mode suppression does not capture all relevant physics and should be used with care.
