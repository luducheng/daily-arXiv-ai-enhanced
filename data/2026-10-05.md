### [1] [Full orbital solution and dynamical masses for a new shell-Be + sdOB binary AN Col](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02367)
*R. Klement,Th. Rivinius,A. C. Carciofi,A. Chaturvedi,D. R. Gies,A. C. Fonseca Silva,P. R. Querido*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This study presents a full 3D orbital solution, dynamical masses, and dynamical parallax for the Be + sdOB binary AN Col, combining GRAVITY near-IR interferometry and double-lined spectroscopy, after disentangling the Be disk's ~5.1 yr oscillation cycle from the ~87.7-day orbital signal.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: AN Col is a shell Be star exhibiting transient spectral features from a hot, evolved companion on a months-long orbit. If confirmed as a post-mass-transfer spun-up Be + stripped sdOB binary, it joins a growing category of such systems. Characterizing the orbit yields dynamical masses, providing a rare anchor for binary-evolution and mass-transfer models.

Method: A prerequisite step characterizes the disk-wide density oscillation cycle responsible for the dominant spectroscopic cycles, allowing separation of weaker periodic orbital variations. Near-IR interferometric time series from GRAVITY confirm the companion and map the relative astrometric orbit. Using the known orbital period, spectral lines tracing each stellar component's orbit are identified and radial velocities measured to obtain a double-lined spectroscopic orbit. Combining interferometry and spectroscopy yields the full 3D orbital solution.

Result: The disk completed three full oscillation cycles since 2010, with a cycle length of ~5.1 yr. The ~87.7-day orbit is compatible with edge-on orientation, but the absence of eclipses in TESS light curves limits orbital inclination to ≤88.4°. Derived masses: M_Be = 8.39 ± 0.67 M_sun and M_sdOB = 0.81 ± 0.22 M_sun. The estimated past mass-transfer efficiency is unexpectedly high, consistent with other Be + sdOB binaries. Detection of weak He II 4686 absorption and other He I lines from the companion indicates an effective temperature near 30 kK and slow rotation.

Conclusion: AN Col is confirmed as a post-mass-transfer Be + sdOB binary, with a complete 3D orbit and dynamical masses obtained via combined interferometry and spectroscopy. Its unexpectedly high mass-transfer efficiency reinforces a pattern seen in other Be + sdOB systems, and the companion is a hot (~30 kK), slowly rotating sdOB star.

Abstract: AN~Col is a shell Be star that has shown transient spectral features caused by a hot, evolved companion on a months-long orbit. If confirmed, this would place it among the growing category of post-mass-transfer spun-up Be + stripped subdwarf OB-type (sdOB) binaries. Characterizing the binary orbit enables obtaining dynamical masses, to provide a rare anchor for models of binary evolution and mass transfer. We present a full 3-dimensional orbital solution, dynamical masses, and dynamical parallax, for the newly discovered Be + sdOB binary AN~Col. As a prerequisite to obtain a reliable orbital solution, we need to characterize the disk-wide density oscillation cycle that gives rise to the dominant spectroscopic cycles in order to separate the weaker periodic orbital variations. We use near-IR interferometry time series from GRAVITY to confirm the companion and to map the relative astrometric orbit. With the knowledge of the orbital period, we identify spectral lines that trace the orbit of each of the two stellar components and we measure their radial velocities to obtain a double-lined spectroscopic orbit. Combining interferometry and spectroscopy, we derive the full 3-dimensional orbital solution. The disk has completed three full oscillation cycles since 2010, with a cycle length of ~5.1 yr. The ~87.7-day orbit is compatible with edge-on orientation, but the lack of eclipses in TESS light curves limits the orbital inclination to 88.4$^{\circ}$ or less. The resulting masses are $M_{\rm Be} = 8.39\pm0.67$ Msun and $M_{\rm sdOB} = 0.81\pm0.22$ Msun. As in other Be + sdOB binaries, the estimated efficiency of the past mass transfer is unexpectedly high. Detection of a weak \spec{He}{ii}{4686} absorption and other \ion{He}{i} lines from the companion indicates that its effective temperature is close to 30 kK, and that it is a slow rotator. ### [2] [The 200-300 GHz Survey for 18 Class II Disks in the Ophiuchus Star-Forming Complex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02619)
*Chia-Ying Chung,Hauyu Baobab Liu,Chuan-Yung Lin,Linjing Feng,Sean M. Andrews,Mark A. Gurwell,Melvyn Wright*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: SMA 198-310 GHz observations of 18 Ophiuchus Class II disks yield a spectral index α₂₀₀₋₃₀₀ ≈ 2.3, similar to Taurus-Auriga compact disks; the near-2 index indicates optically thick dust thermal emission dominates, linking submillimeter luminosity and apparent disk size.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The paper aims to characterize the millimeter/submillimeter emission properties (spectral indices, flux densities) of Class II protoplanetary disks in Ophiuchus and compare them with the Taurus-Auriga sample to understand what governs continuum emission at ≳200 GHz and whether disk size or luminosity reflects dust mass depletion in young clusters.

Method: Measured 198-310 GHz flux densities of 18 Class II protoplanetary disks in the Ophiuchus star-forming complex using the Submillimeter Array (SMA), deriving the ~200-300 GHz spectral indices (α₂₀₀₋₃₀₀). Combined these with 48 spatially compact Class II disks from a previous SMA survey of Taurus-Auriga, then computed the Pearson correlation coefficient between α₂₀₀₋₃₀₀ and the 250 GHz flux density (F₂₅₀ GHz) and examined correlation with the R₉₅% radius.

Result: The Ophiuchus disks have α₂₀₀₋₃₀₀ = 2.3⁺⁰·²₋₀.₅, with emission properties very similar to the 48 compact Taurus-Auriga Class II disks. For the combined Ophiuchus + Taurus-Auriga sample, the Pearson correlation coefficient between α₂₀₀₋₃₀₀ and F₂₅₀ GHz is 0.27 (p = 0.02). No convincing correlation is found between α₂₀₀₋₃₀₀ and the R₉₅% radius.

Conclusion: The α₂₀₀₋₃₀₀ values near 2 in spatially compact disks suggest that at ≳200 GHz the continuum emission is dominated by optically thick dust thermal emission, independent of F₂₅₀ GHz and R₉₅%. Consequently, (sub)millimeter luminosity and apparent disk size are positively correlated, as seen in many resolved Class II disk surveys. In young (~1-2 Myr) clusters, Class II disks with smaller radii or lower (sub)millimeter luminosities are not necessarily those whose dust mass budget has been consumed or dispersed.

Abstract: We measured the 198-310 GHz flux densities of 18 Class II protoplanetary disks in the Ophiuchus star-forming complex using the Submillimeter Array (SMA). The $\sim$200-300 GHz spectral indices ($α_{200-300}$) of these disks are $2.3^{+0.2}_{-0.5}$. In this observed frequency range, emission properties of the Ophiuchus disks are very similar to the 48 spatially compact Class II disks in the Taurus-Auriga region observed in a previous SMA survey. Combining the Ophiuchus and Taurus-Auriga samples, the Pearson correlation coefficient between $α_{200-300}$ and the flux density at 250 GHz ($F_{250\,\mathrm{GHz}}$) is 0.27 ($p=0.02$). We do not find a convincing correlation between $α_{200-300}$ and the $R_{95\%}$ radius of the disks. Our interpretation for the $α_{200-300}$ values being close to 2 in the spatially compact disks is that at $\gtrsim 200$ GHz frequencies, the continuum emission in these disks is dominated by optically thick dust thermal emission, independent of $F_{250\,\mathrm{GHz}}$ and $R_{95\%}$. As a result, the (sub)millimeter luminosity and the apparent disk size are positively correlated, which has been found in numerous resolved emission surveys of Class II disks. In young clusters with ages of $\sim$1-2 Myr, Class II disks with smaller radii or lower (sub)millimeter luminosities are not necessarily those in which the dust mass budget has been consumed or dispersed.

### [3] [Mesoscale Turbulence in Type Ia Supernova Deflagrations II. Evidence for Fuel Preheat Due to Flame Thermal Expansion from Lagrangian Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02677)
*Ryan Learn,Andrey Zhiglo,Tomasz Plewa*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Multidimensional simulations with Lagrangian tracer particles reveal how unsteady thermonuclear flames in Type Ia supernovae precondition nearby fuel via flame-driven expansion and weak acoustic waves, systematically shortening carbon ignition times.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Recent work (Brooker+25) showed that fuel ignition times are systematically shortened near Rayleigh–Taylor unstable deflagration fronts in Type Ia supernovae, but the underlying physical mechanism was unclear. This study aims to identify the thermodynamic origin of this ignition-time variability in partially degenerate carbon–oxygen plasma.

Method: The authors perform multidimensional simulations of fuel near the flame front coupled with Lagrangian tracer-particle analysis to track the thermodynamic evolution of individual fuel parcels. They compare low- and high-density models, focusing on flame-driven expansion and ignition-time variability. Nuclear burning is disabled in the analyzed fuel to isolate thermodynamic effects.

Result: The most strongly preconditioned fuel parcels—a minority of the fuel layer—undergo approximately pressure-constrained expansion with modest density reductions and temperature increases from ≈2×10^8 K to ≈7×10^8 K (≈13 to 63 per cent above unperturbed fuel temperature) between low- and high-density models. Despite modest thermodynamic perturbations, the strong temperature sensitivity of carbon ignition produces substantial ignition-time shortening.

Conclusion: A two-step mechanism is proposed: flame thermal expansion drives the fuel away from hydrostatic equilibrium, and subsequent restoration of pressure balance by weak acoustic waves redistributes internal energy from degenerate to thermal components, raising temperature and causing localized thermodynamic preconditioning. Unsteady thermonuclear flames can thus shorten ignition times ahead of the flame over distances exceeding the laminar flame thickness. Whether this leads to secondary ignition or contributes to deflagration-to-detonation transition remains untested here.

Abstract: Thermonuclear flame fronts in Type Ia supernovae develop hydrodynamic instabilities that generate turbulent mesoscale flow and substantial local thermodynamic variability. Recent work by \citet{Brooker+25} demonstrated systematic shortening of fuel ignition times near Rayleigh--Taylor unstable deflagration fronts, but the physical origin remained unclear. We investigate the thermodynamic evolution of fuel near the flame front using multidimensional simulations and Lagrangian tracer-particle analysis, focusing on flame-driven expansion and ignition-time variability in partially degenerate carbon--oxygen plasma. Tracer trajectories show that the most strongly preconditioned fuel parcels near the flame front, which form a minority of the fuel layer, undergo approximately pressure-constrained expansion with modest density reductions and temperature increases of $\approx 2\times10^{8}$~K to $\approx7\times10^{8}$~K ($\approx13$ to $63$ per cent above the unperturbed fuel temperature) between the low- and high-density models. Despite moderate thermodynamic perturbations, the strong temperature sensitivity of carbon ignition produces substantial shortening of ignition times. The results indicate a two-step mechanism: flame thermal expansion perturbs the fuel away from hydrostatic equilibrium, while restoration of pressure balance by weak acoustic waves redistributes internal energy from degenerate to thermal components. This raises the fuel temperature and produces localized thermodynamic preconditioning. These results suggest that unsteady thermonuclear flames can shorten ignition times ahead of the flame, influencing fuel over distances larger than the laminar flame thickness. Whether this preconditioning leads to secondary ignition or contributes to deflagration-to-detonation transition remains untested here, as nuclear burning is disabled in the analyzed fuel.

### [4] [A New Analysis Tool for Radial Pulsations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02733)
*Hiroyasu Ando*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: A wave-energy relation is introduced as a new tool for linear radial pulsation analysis, decomposed into kinetic and three potential energies, and applied to massive ZAMS models to show that strange modes leak significant wave energy.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Conventional modal analysis of stellar pulsations lacks a framework that explicitly tracks how non-adiabatic processes and wave leakage redistribute energy among modes. The paper aims to provide such a diagnostic to distinguish normal modes from strange modes in massive stars.

Method: The authors formulate a wave-energy relation comprising kinetic energy, acoustic-potential energy, dynamical-potential energy, and entropy-potential energy. The relation captures changes in wave energy due to non-adiabatic processes and wave-energy leakage. It is applied to modal analysis of adiabatic pulsations in Zero Age Main Sequence models of 10, 20, and 30 solar masses.

Result: Ordinary modes are well trapped in the interior cavity with negligible wave-energy leakage. In contrast, strange modes (S-modes) show steep increases in eigenfunctions and kinetic energy toward the stellar surface, accompanied by significant wave-energy leakage, with their kinetic energy no longer confined to the exterior cavity.

Conclusion: The wave-energy relation is an effective diagnostic for tracing energy leakage and trapping in radial pulsations, clearly separating ordinary modes from strange modes in massive ZAMS models.

Abstract: Wave-energy relation is introduced as a new analysis tool for linear radial pulsations. Wave energy consists of kinetic energy, acoustic-potential energy, dynamical-potential energy, and entropy-potential energy. The relation describes changes in the wave energy caused by non-adiabatic processes and by leakage of wave energy. It is applied to modal analysis of adiabatic pulsations for the massive Zero Age Main Sequence models (10, 20, and 30 solar masses). The ordinary modes are well trapped in the interior cavity without any significant wave-energy leakage. On the other hand, the strange modes (S-modes) exhibit the steep increases in the eigenfunctions and kinetic energy toward the stellar surface and the significant wave-energy leakage. The kinetic energy of S-modes is no longer confined in the exterior cavity.

### [5] [Formation and Eruption of a Vortex-driven Magnetic Flux Rope in the Simulated Quiet Sun](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02746)
*Zhizhen Ye,Feng Chen*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: A radiative MHD simulation reveals the formation, twisting, and stealthy eruption of a supergranular-scale magnetic flux rope in the quiet Sun, driven by a persistent vortex at a supergranule convergence point.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Magnetic flux ropes are central to understanding flares and coronal mass ejections in active regions, yet their characteristics in the quiet Sun remain poorly understood.

Method: Radiative MHD simulation spanning from the upper convection zone to the corona; a clustering method groups closed field lines by connectivity, further assembled into 8 persistent cluster assemblies (CAs) representing a flux rope and ambient magnetic structures.

Result: A persistent counterclockwise vortex at the supergranule convergence point (the positive footpoint) continuously injects helicity into a magnetic flux tube, wrapping ambient structures around it. Within about one hour, the flux tube becomes a strongly twisted flux rope and erupts, showing current sheet structures similar to active-region eruptions, but with much weaker mass ejection and heating. Synthetic EUV images show mostly dimming from density rarefaction in the expanding flux rope.

Conclusion: The work clarifies the complex, dynamic evolution of quiet-Sun flux ropes and suggests similar real-Sun events may be underestimated because of their stealth behavior.

Abstract: Magnetic flux ropes (MFRs) are key structures for understanding flares and coronal mass ejections in active regions, but their characteristics in the quiet Sun remain poorly understood. With a radiative MHD simulation spanning from the upper convection zone to the corona, we analyze the formation and eruption of a supergranular-scale flux rope. We use a clustering method to group the closed field lines by their connectivity. The clusters are gathered further into 8 persistent cluster assemblies (CAs) that correspond to a flux rope and ambient magnetic structures. A persistent counterclockwise vortex is maintained at the converging point of the supergranules, i.e. the positive footpoint of the flux rope. The vortex continuously injects helicity into a magnetic flux tube and wraps ambient magnetic structures around the central flux tube. On a time scale of one hour, the flux tube evolves to a strongly twisted flux rope and erupts. The eruption exhibits similar current sheet structures as inferred from the flux rope eruption in active regions; however, the mass ejection and heating are much weaker and give rise to very insignificant observable features. The synthetic EUV images show mostly dimming features caused by density rarefaction in the expanding flux rope. This work helps elucidate the dynamic and complex evolution of flux ropes formed in the quiet Sun and suggests that similar events in the real Sun may have been underestimated due to their stealth behavior.

### [7] [The origin of dispersion in the wind mass-loss rate--luminosity relation of massive O stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03431)
*Jiri Krticka,Iva Krtickova*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Tailored wind models for individual Large Magellanic Cloud O stars show that dependence of mass-loss rate on parameters other than luminosity (effective temperature, mass, metallicity) partly explains the scatter in the observed mass-loss rate–luminosity relation, though residual dispersion suggests wind variability also contributes.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Hot star winds are radiatively driven by line transitions of heavy elements, so mass-loss rate is expected to scale mostly with luminosity. Observations of O stars confirm this scaling but with substantial unexplained scatter. The authors investigate whether this scatter arises from sensitivity of mass-loss rate to stellar parameters beyond luminosity, such as effective temperature, mass, and metallicity.

Method: Using a global wind code that computes the radiative force from the solution of the radiative transfer equation in the comoving frame, the authors calculated tailored wind models for stellar parameters corresponding to selected individual O stars in the Large Magellanic Cloud, with parameters taken from a previous analysis.

Result: Wind models calculated for particular stellar parameters reproduce the observed mass-loss rates better than simple scaling relations derived from model grids, indicating that dependence of mass-loss rate on stellar parameters other than luminosity can explain part of the dispersion in the mass-loss rate–luminosity relation. However, some dispersion remains.

Conclusion: The sensitivity of mass-loss rate to parameters besides luminosity (effective temperature, mass, metallicity) partly accounts for the scatter in the hot star mass-loss rate–luminosity relation. The residual dispersion that cannot be explained this way likely points to wind variability as an additional source of the scatter.

Abstract: Stellar winds of hot stars are accelerated by the radiative force that mainly comes from the line transitions of heavy elements. As a result, the wind mass-loss rate is expected to scale mostly with the stellar luminosity. This scaling clearly emerges from the observational analysis of O stars and their winds, but with substantial scatter. The origin of this scatter is unclear. Part of the scatter of the observational relation between the hot star wind mass-loss rate and luminosity may originate from the sensitivity of the mass-loss rate to other stellar parameters besides the luminosity, such as the effective temperature, mass, and metallicity. We tested this possibility using our wind models. We calculated tailored wind models for stellar parameters corresponding to selected individual O stars from the Large Magellanic Cloud as derived from a previous analysis. To model the wind, we used our global wind code that calculates the radiative force from the solution of the radiative transfer equation in the comoving frame. Wind models calculated for particular stellar parameters reproduce the observational mass-loss rates better than simple scaling relations derived from model grids. This indicates that the dependence of the mass-loss rate on the other stellar parameters except the luminosity might explain part of the dispersion in the relation between the mass-loss rate and luminosity. However, some dispersion still remains, which might indicate that wind variability is another source of the dispersion.
