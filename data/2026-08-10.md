### [1] [Spectroscopic follow-up of compact object binary candidates from Gaia DR3: White dwarfs, neutron stars, black holes, and the parallax zeropoint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06453)

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Gaia DR3 delivered hundreds of thousands of binary orbits, with several samples proposed to contain black holes, neutron stars, and white dwarfs. However, a substantial fraction of these astrometric candidates may be spurious or misidentified, so systematic spectroscopic verification is essential to determine the true purity of these samples and to identify genuine dark companions.

Method: The authors conducted a systematic multi-epoch spectroscopic follow-up program of 227 sources, combining archival data with new observations from TRES and FEROS spectrographs over four years. They obtained 1292 high-quality RVs at ~50 m/s precision, with at least 10 epochs for 60 sources. They performed joint fitting of Gaia astrometry and RVs to constrain orbital parameters, component masses, and flux ratios, enabling verification of whether companions are truly dark. This also enabled parallax-independent distance measurements for systems with dark companions.

Conclusion: Spectroscopic follow-up is essential to separate genuine compact-object binaries from spurious astrometric solutions. Abstract: Astrometry and radial velocities (RVs) from Gaia DR3 yielded orbits for hundreds of thousands of binary systems, including several samples proposed to contain black holes (BHs), neutron stars (NSs), and white dwarfs (WDs). We present results of a systematic spectroscopic follow-up program targeting these objects. Beginning with a sample of 227 sources, we used a combination of archival data and many-epoch spectroscopic follow-up to characterize more than 200. We obtained 1292 high-quality RVs over a period of four years using the TRES and FEROS spectrographs, achieving a typical precision of 50 m/s and at least 10 RVs for 60 sources. We use these data to test the Gaia orbital solutions and tighten constraints on orbital parameters and component masses. Joint fitting of astrometry and RVs allows us to directly constrain flux ratios, verifying that undetected companions are genuinely dark. We show that tight WD+WD binaries may masquerade as NSs within this sample. The spectroscopic candidates have lower purity: ~50% have spurious solutions, and a majority of the rest are post-mass-transfer binaries or hierarchical triples. Joint astrometry+RV fits of binaries with dark companions yield direct, parallax-independent distance measurements. Using 40 such systems, we measure the Gaia DR3 parallax zeropoint for astrometric orbital solutions. We find $Z=-0.0362\pm0.0053$ mas, consistent with the single-star zeropoint for sources of similar color and magnitude. These results will guide the selection of cleaner candidate samples from Gaia DR4, where a longer observing baseline will enable discovery of many more compact object binaries. RV follow-up will remain important for confirming individual systems, particularly those with extreme parameters.

### [2] [Multi-wavelength synthesis of a flux rope-trapped mini-prominence eruption and post-flare coronal rain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06478)

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper uses forward modeling of a 2.5D MHD simulation to reproduce small-scale coronal eruptive phenomena (homologous flux rope eruptions, mini-prominence formation, post-flare coronal rain) and generates synthetic multi-wavelength diagnostics (EUV, UV, Hα, Si IV) that provide observable signatures to guide future high-resolution observations of these processes.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Small-scale eruptive phenomena in the solar corona, such as miniature flux ropes with embedded cool plasma condensations, remain poorly understood despite growing high-resolution observational data. The authors aim to bridge the gap between simulations and observations by producing synthetic diagnostics that can be directly compared with future observations.

Method: The authors perform forward modeling based on a 2.5D MHD simulation that captures homologous flux rope eruptions, in-situ condensation forming a mini-prominence, and post-flare coronal rain. Synthetic diagnostics are generated using optically-thin EUV and UV emission models, plus non-LTE radiative transfer for the Hα line. Spectral synthesis of Si IV 1402.77 Å is also performed to analyze velocities and thermal structuring.

Result: EUV synthetic emission shows flux ropes as bright rim-like structures; UV diagnostics reveal a bright region co-spatial with a dark core from embedded cool plasma (tens of kK), identifying an erupting mini-prominence. Si IV synthesis shows upward motion of the mini-filament and two predominant velocity components during eruption. Later, thermal instability produces coronal rain (~10^4 K) with EUV brightening at rain blob downstream (localized compressional heating). Hα shows enhanced absorption and red-shifted downflows up to ~23 km/s, while Si IV shows maximum downflow of ~50 km/s, revealing internal thermodynamic and kinematic structuring.

Conclusion: The synthetic diagnostics provide clear multi-wavelength signatures for guiding future high-resolution observations, and highlight that small-scale reconnection-driven processes are important in shaping the multi-thermal structure (MK to kK) of the solar corona.

Abstract: Small-scale eruptive phenomena in the solar corona including miniature flux ropes and associated cool plasma condensations are not fully understood despite increasing high-resolution observations. We perform forward modeling based on a 2.5D MHD simulation capturing homologous flux rope eruptions, in-situ condensation leading to a mini-prominence, and subsequent post-flare coronal rain. The H$α$ spectral synthesis shows enhanced absorption signatures and red-shifted profiles corresponding to downflows of the coronal rain blobs up to $\approx 23$ km s$^{-1}$, whereas the Si IV 1402.77 A spectral profile shows the maximum downflow velocity of $\approx50$ km s$^{-1}$, highlighting the evidence of thermodynamic and kinematic structuring within the falling rain blobs. The synthetic diagnostics provide clear, multi-wavelength signatures that can guide future high-resolution observations, and highlight the importance of small-scale reconnection-driven processes in shaping the multi-thermal structure (between MK to kK) of the solar corona.

### [3] [The Maunder Model and Catalog: Stellar Rotation, Bimodal Activity, and Magnetic Braking in Kepler Main-Sequence Stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06604)
*Ilay Kamai,Lavi Somers,Hagai B. Perets*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: The Maunder is a machine learning pipeline producing a rotation-period catalog for 148,746 main-sequence Kepler stars, using a hybrid self-supervised + supervised training objective with conformalized quantile regression to provide calibrated uncertainty intervals. It identifies bimodal rotation signals in 21.5% of stars, uses APOGEE vsini data to show longer modes are true rotations (revealing periodogram alias failures), and filters to a reliable subset of 119,428 stars that enables studies of metallicity-dependent magnetic braking, the Kraft break, gyrochronology, and hierarchical triples.

Motivation: To overcome single-catalog systematics and the simulation-to-reality gap in rotation period estimation, providing a reliable, statistically calibrated rotation catalog for the largest main-sequence population in the Kepler field.

Method: A hybrid training objective combining a joint-embedding self-supervised loss on all light curves with a supervised loss on cross-catalog consensus labels; multi-scale time- and frequency-domain inputs processed over rolling windows; conformalized quantile regression for calibrated predictive intervals; validation against APOGEE vsini measurements.

Result: Rotation catalog for 148,746 main-sequence stars; 31,953 stars (21.5%) show bimodal rotation signals with longer modes being the true rotation for non-harmonic bimodals; calibrated confidence filtering yields a reliable subset of 119,428 stars; enabled recovery of metallicity-rotation relation, Kraft break features, gyrochronology sequences, and hierarchical triple candidates.

Conclusion: The Maunder provides the largest reliable rotation-period catalog for Kepler main-sequence stars, exposing systematic alias failures in classical periodograms and enabling population-level studies of rotation-based stellar phenomena.

Abstract: We present The Maunder, a machine learning pipeline and resulting catalog of rotation periods for 148,746 main-sequence stars in the Kepler field. To overcome single-catalog systematics and the simulation-to-reality gap, our architecture employs a hybrid training objective: a joint-embedding self-supervised loss applied to all light curves, combined with a supervised loss trained strictly on cross-catalog consensus labels. By processing multi-scale time- and frequency-domain inputs over rolling windows, the model leverages conformalized quantile regression to output calibrated predictive intervals, providing statistically robust per-star rotation uncertainty metrics. This rolling-window inference reveals that 31,953 stars (21.5$\%$) exhibit bimodal rotational signals. By incorporating APOGEE $v \sin i$ measurements, we demonstrate that for distinct (non-harmonic) bimodals, the longer mode represents the true rotation, exposing a systematic failure mode wherein classical single-pass periodograms lock onto shorter aliases. Filtering by our calibrated confidence intervals yields a highly reliable subset of 119,428 stars. The catalog resolves various rotation-related phenomena: the metallicity dependence of rotation at fixed stellar mass, pointing on the role of metallicity in magnetic braking processes; tracing equatorial velocity and specific angular momentum directly across the Kraft break; recovery of empirical gyrochronology sequences and identification of hierarchical triple candidates among the synchronized-binary population. \emph{The Maunder} provides reliable rotation periods for the largest main-sequence population in \textit{Kepler}, allowing for population-level studies of rotation-based phenomena.

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: The first detection of the λ Boo phenomenon (refractory element depletion with normal volatile abundances) in a massive early B-type star, HD 37356 in Ori OB1c, is reported using hybrid non-LTE model-atmosphere techniques with neural network emulation and MCMC analysis.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: λ Boo stars are chemically peculiar A-type stars exhibiting refractory element depletions and normal volatile abundances, but their formation channels are not fully established. Detecting this phenomenon in more massive stars provides constraints on formation mechanisms and star-disc-planet interactions.

Method: High-quality spectrum analysed using a hybrid non-LTE model-atmosphere technique. Atmospheric parameters and elemental abundances determined using neural networks to emulate synthetic spectra, coupled with a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm. Fundamental parameters derived from Gaia data and stellar evolution models.

Result: HD 37356 closely resembles the Morgan-Keenan standard γ Peg except for systematically weaker refractory element lines. Stellar parameters are similar to γ Peg. Volatile abundances match cosmic standard values, while refractory species are depleted by 0.25-0.45 dex. The lower iron abundance explains the different pulsational behaviour compared to γ Peg.

Conclusion: Only the accretion of gas depleted in refractory elements from the circumstellar disc during star formation appears feasible among the proposed λ Boo formation channels. This requires HD 37356 to be a true slow rotator to prevent the abundance pattern from being erased by internal mixing processes such as meridional circulation.

Abstract: Chemically peculiar A-type stars of the $λ$ Boo class are characterised by depletions of refractory elements and normal volatile species. Their formation channel(s) have not yet been fully established, but they provide insight into star-disc-planet interactions during star formation, star-planet interactions in later system evolution, or interactions between stars and the interstellar medium. We report the first detection of the $λ$ Boo phenomenon in a massive early B-type star, HD 37356, a member of Ori OB1c. We analysed a high-quality spectrum using a hybrid non-LTE model-atmosphere technique. We determined atmospheric parameters and elemental abundances using neural networks to emulate synthetic spectra, coupled with a Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm. We also derived the fundamental parameters of the star using Gaia data and stellar evolution models. HD 37356 closely resembles the Morgan-Keenan standard $γ$ Peg, closely agreeing in spectral signatures except for the lines of refractory elements, which are systematically weaker. The quantitative analysis yields similar stellar parameters. The abundances of volatile elements are compatible with cosmic standard abundances, while refractory species are depleted by 0.25 to 0.45dex. In particular, the lower iron abundance explains the different pulsational behaviour of HD 37356 compared to $γ$ Peg. Of the different formation channels for $λ$ Boo stars, only the accretion of gas depleted in refractory elements from the circumstellar disc during star formation appears to be feasible in this case. This scenario requires HD 37356 to be a true slow rotator in order to prevent the abundance pattern from being erased by internal mixing processes such as meridional circulation.

### [6] [Instability and Angular Momentum Transfer in Thick Circumbinary Disks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07159)

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper uses the DISCO code to simulate 2D hydrodynamic equal-mass circumbinary disks with isothermal equation of state, focusing on eccentric instabilities in thicker disks (Mach number ≤ 14).   <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand how disk thickness and viscosity affect the eccentric instability in circumbinary disks and how this instability influences angular momentum transport and binary migration behavior.

Method: 2D hydrodynamic simulations using the DISCO code for an equal-mass circular restricted binary with an isothermal circumbinary disk, measuring the growth rate of the disk cavity's eccentricity and angular momentum transport from accretion across a range of Mach numbers (≤ 14).

Result: Once the eccentric instability saturates, the torque on the binary rapidly flips sign, transitioning the binary from inward to outward migration. Saturation time is non-linearly dependent on Mach number, with thicker disks requiring longer evolution times. No eccentric instability is found for Mach ≲ 5, resulting in robust inward migration for very thick disks (h/r ≳ 0.2). Results are consistent with community results for Mach ≥ 10.

Conclusion: The saturation of the disk's eccentric instability is directly linked to a reversal in binary migration direction. Disk thickness determines whether the instability develops at all, with very thick disks lacking the instability and thus producing inward migration, while thinner disks exhibit eccentricity saturation and outward migration.

Abstract: Utilizing the DISCO code we solve the 2D hydrodynamic equations for an equal mass circular restricted binary with an isothermal circumbinary disk (CBD). This work explores the eccentric instability in the CBD and angular momentum transfer, and their dependence on disk thickness and viscosity. The focus is specifically on thicker CBDs, with Mach number, ($\mathcal{M}\leq 14$). The growth rate of the disk cavity's eccentricity and the angular momentum transport from accretion is calculated as diagnostics for binary-disk morphology evolution. Calculations of CBD torques must run long enough to allow saturation of the disk's eccentric instability. The torque on the binary rapidly flips sign once the instability saturates. Thus we connect eccentricity saturation to a reversal in migration behavior for the binary. The binary experiences inward migration while the disk is in an evolving state, and outward migration once the disk eccentricity has saturated. The saturation time was found to be non-linearly dependent on Mach number and thicker CBDs require longer evolution time for a quasi-steady state to appear. ### [8] [White Dwarf Kicks via Episodic Mass Ejection from Red Giant Stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07455)
*Jim Fuller*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: White dwarf kicks of ~1 km/s arise from asymmetric, episodic mass loss during the red giant phase, where numerous (~10^4) small ejection events impart randomly oriented kicks (~5 m/s each) that sum to ~0.5 km/s. Higher-mass WDs receive larger kicks. In binaries, these kicks can unbind wide systems (a ≳ 10^3 AU) and drive eccentricities toward unity, potentially causing collisions, luminous transients, and eccentric common envelope events.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Recent observations have detected ~1 km/s kicks imparted to white dwarfs when their red giant progenitor envelopes are expelled, and the physical origin of these kicks is not yet established. Understanding the kick mechanism is important for interpreting WD kinematics, binary orbital evolution, and their implications for stellar populations.

Method: The authors propose that asymmetric and episodic mass loss from red giant stars generates the observed WD kicks. They use simple hydrodynamic models of red giant mass loss to estimate per-event mass ejection (~10^-4 M_sun) and per-kick velocities (~5 m/s). They combine N ~ 10^4 stochastic events to compute net kicks via random walk scaling (v_k,tot ≈ √N v_k). They also develop analytic models for binary orbital evolution under a stochastic series of kicks and compare them with numerical integrations.

Result: The model reproduces total kicks of ~0.5 km/s, consistent with observations, and predicts substantially larger net kicks for higher-mass WDs. For nominal mass ejection parameters, wide binaries (a ≳ 10^3 AU) can become unbound, with the unbound fraction increasing with separation and WD mass. Orbital eccentricities can approach unity, leading to stellar collisions that may produce luminous transients and eccentric common envelope events.

Conclusion: Stochastic asymmetric mass loss during the red giant phase provides a plausible mechanism for the observed WD kicks. This kick mechanism has important dynamical consequences for wide binaries, including unbinding and the production of eccentric-orbit merger events, with implications for luminous transients and common envelope phenomena.

Abstract: Recent observations have found evidence that white dwarf (WD) stars receive a kick of $\sim$1 km/s as the envelopes of their red giant progenitors are expelled. We show that these kicks can arise from asymmetric and episodic mass loss of red giant stars. Based on simple hydrodynamic models of red giant mass loss, each mass ejection event likely expels of order $\sim \! 10^{-4} \, M_\odot$, imparting a small and randomly oriented kick to the star, with the net kick to the WD arising from a combination of many $(N \sim 10^4)$ events. Each kick is of order $v_k \sim 5 \, {\rm m/s}$, with a total kick of order $v_{\rm k,tot} \approx \sqrt{N} v_k \sim 0.5 \, {\rm km/s}$. We predict substantially larger net kicks for higher mass WDs. We also model the orbital evolution of binary stars experiencing a stochastic series of kicks, developing analytic models to explain numerical integrations. The orbital eccentricities can also approach unity, causing stellar collisions that may result in luminous transients and eccentric common envelope events.
