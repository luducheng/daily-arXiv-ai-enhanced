### [1] [Chemical evolution of Na, Mg, and Al in the Galactic bulge from UVES data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01801)
*R. P. Nunes,B. Barbuy,C. Chiappini,A. Friaça,M. C. Jelin,V. Hill,M. Zoccali,A. Renzini,A. Pérez-Villegas,H. Ernandes,S. O. Souza,M. J. Castro*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper investigates Na, Mg, and Al abundances in 55 Galactic bulge red giants using high-resolution UVES/VLT spectra and spectrum synthesis with the Turbospectrum code. The authors find lower Mg and Al abundances at the metal-rich end than previous studies but confirm robust Na overabundances in some metal-rich stars, which is difficult to reconcile with standard chemical evolution models. Possible explanations include metallicity-dependent massive star yields, AGB enrichment, or second-generation stars evaporated from globular clusters (indicated by a Na-O anti-correlation).

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The Galactic bulge formation is poorly understood, with multiple stellar populations proposed (bar-driven, inner-disk, older spheroidal). Chemical abundances of odd-Z elements (Na, Al) and Mg provide critical constraints, especially at high metallicity where their behavior is debated. The study aims to robustly determine these abundances to test chemical evolution scenarios.

Method: Abundances of Na, Mg, and Al were re-derived for 55 bulge red giants previously analyzed in the literature. The analysis uses high-resolution UVES spectra from the ESO Very Large Telescope and employs spectrum synthesis with the Turbospectrum code, incorporating updated atomic and molecular line lists. Robustness of abundances was tested against variations in adopted stellar parameters.

Result: The analysis yields somewhat lower Mg and Al abundances at the metal-rich end compared to previous studies, while a fraction of metal-rich stars still show significant Na enhancements that persist across different sets of stellar parameters, indicating robustness. A Na-O anti-correlation appears to occur for the metal-rich ([Fe/H]>0) stars.

Conclusion: The persistent Na enhancement at high metallicity is difficult to reconcile with standard chemical evolution models. Abstract: The formation of the Galactic bulge remains incompletely understood, with evidence pointing to different stellar populations, including a bar-driven component, an inner-disk population, and an older spheroidal component. Chemical abundances provide critical constraints on the origin of these populations, particularly for odd-Z elements such as Na and Al, as well as Mg, whose behaviour at high metallicity is still a matter of debate. We aim to investigate the presence of overabundances of Na, Mg, and Al in Galactic bulge stars, with particular emphasis on the metal-rich regime, and to evaluate their consistency with predictions from chemical evolution models. We re-derived the abundances of Na, Mg, and Al for a sample of 55 bulge red giants previously analysed in the literature. Our study is based on high-resolution UVES spectra obtained with the ESO Very Large Telescope and employs spectrum synthesis using the Turbospectrum code, with updated atomic and molecular line lists. We find somewhat lower abundances of Mg and Al at the metal-rich end than previous studies, while a fraction of the metal-rich stars still exhibit significant Na enhancements. ### [2] [Mass-dependent multiplicity fraction in low mass stars revealed by Gaia astrometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01822)
*Ben Pennell,Hans-Walter Rix,Kareem El-Badry,Sahar Shahaf,Johanna Müller-Horn,Jiadong Li*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper uses Gaia DR3 astrometric diagnostics (accelerations, jerks, RUWE) alongside orbit solutions, combined with the Gaiamock emulator in a forward-modelling framework, to constrain stellar multiplicity across orbital periods for 366,027 M-dwarf primaries (0.2–0.75 M☉, 50–200 pc). They find the multiplicity fraction is roughly constant at ~45% from solar-type stars down to 0.4 M☉, then drops to ~15% by 0.2 M☉, revising the previously assumed monotonically declining mass–multiplicity relation.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To constrain binary/multiple star populations across orbital periods using the rich astrometric data of Gaia DR3, particularly to measure the multiplicity fraction of M-dwarfs on a much larger scale than previous studies and test the mass–multiplicity relationship.

Method: The authors build a forward-modelling framework using the Gaiamock emulator, which simulates Gaia astrometric signatures. Rather than relying only on the small fraction of full orbit solutions, they exploit all available astrometric diagnostics from Gaia DR3—on-sky accelerations, jerks, and the single-star goodness-of-fit measure RUWE—which together are sensitive to a much wider range of orbital periods than orbit solutions alone. This framework is applied to 366,027 primaries of 0.2–0.75 M☉ between 50 and 200 pc.

Result: For fiducial models of period and mass-ratio distributions, the inferred multiplicity fraction is approximately constant at ~45% from the solar-type regime down to 0.4 M☉, and then drops to ~15% by 0.2 M☉. This revises the conventional picture of a monotonically rising mass–multiplicity relation, showing that upper M-dwarfs behave like solar-type stars rather than continuing a decline.

Conclusion: Gaia DR3 astrometric diagnostics, when combined in a forward model, provide powerful constraints on binary populations over a wide range of orbital periods. The resulting mass–multiplicity relation is not monotonically declining across all stellar masses: the multiplicity fraction plateaus around 45% until 0.4 M☉ before dropping sharply to ~15% toward lower-mass M-dwarfs.

Abstract: Stellar multiplicity at different orbital periods is probed by different techniques: radial velocities at the shortest periods, direct imaging at the longest, and astrometry in between. Gaia DR3 provides unprecedented astrometric information to constrain binary populations with periods of tens to thousands of days. Beyond the small fraction of direct orbit solutions, Gaia publishes coarser astrometric diagnostics for all objects, such as on-sky accelerations, jerks, or the single-star goodness-of-fit measure, RUWE. We show that these diagnostics together are sensitive to a far wider range of orbital periods than orbit solutions alone. Built on the Gaiamock emulator, we develop a forward-modelling framework to constrain rates of multiplicity from Gaia data, using all astrometric solution types. We apply this framework to $366~027$ primaries of $0.2$--$0.75\,M_\odot$ between $50$ and $200$ pc, including vastly more M-dwarfs than previous multiplicity studies. For fiducial models of the period and mass-ratio distributions, the inferred multiplicity fraction is approximately constant at ${\sim}45\%$ from the solar-type regime down to $0.4\,M_\odot$, only then dropping to ${\sim}15\%$ by $0.2\,M_\odot$. This mass-multiplicity relationship revises the conventional picture of a monotonically rising mass--multiplicity relation, and aligns the upper M-dwarf regime with solar-type stars rather than placing it on a continuous decline.

### [3] [Electron acceleration by turbulent reconnection in solar flares](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02151)
*Zining Ren,Xin Cheng,Yulei Wang,Mingde Ding*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper investigates electron acceleration and transport during three-dimensional turbulent reconnection in solar flares by solving Parker's transport equation coupled to high-resolution MHD simulations. Thermal electrons are rapidly accelerated to ~90 keV at both the flare current sheet and loop top, producing power-law energy spectra. Notably, acceleration is driven by turbulence-driven compression structures of various scales rather than termination shocks, especially at the loop top. The findings emphasize the critical role of turbulent reconnection in particle acceleration, with broader implications for high-energy astrophysical phenomena.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Solar flares explosively release magnetic energy in the corona and generate high-energy particles on short timescales, yet the mechanisms and locations of particle acceleration remain unresolved. Understanding how and where electrons are accelerated during flare reconnection is a fundamental open question in heliophysics and astrophysics.

Method: The study couples high-resolution MHD simulations of self-developed three-dimensional turbulent reconnection with the numerical solution of Parker's transport equation to model the acceleration and transport of electrons in the flare environment.

Result: Thermal electrons are rapidly accelerated up to ~90 keV at both the flare current sheet and loop top, with energy spectra exhibiting power-law characteristics. The accelerated electron population is larger at the loop top than at the current sheet, yet spectral indices are similar at both sites and close to observed values. Acceleration is primarily driven by turbulence-driven compression structures of varying scales, some of which form shocks, rather than by a termination shock—particularly at the flare loop top.

Conclusion: Turbulent reconnection plays a crucial role in accelerating electrons during solar flares, challenging the termination shock scenario and providing new insight into particle acceleration and transport that may apply to other high-energy astrophysical phenomena.

Abstract: Solar flares can release magnetic energy explosively in the corona and produce high-energy particles on short timescales. However, how and where these particles are accelerated remains an open question. Here, we investigate the acceleration and transport of electrons during self-developed three-dimensional turbulent reconnection of solar flares by solving Parker's transport equation in the framework of high-resolution MHD simulations. We find that thermal electrons at both the flare current sheet and loop top are rapidly accelerated up to ~90keV, with energy spectra exhibiting a power-law feature. Although the population of accelerated electrons at the flare loop top is larger than that at the current sheet, their spectral indices are similar, close to the values usually observed. More importantly, the acceleration is achieved by turbulence-driven compression structures of various scales rather than the supposed termination shock, particularly at the flare loop top. A portion of compression structures even forms shocks. These results highlight the critical role of turbulent reconnection in accelerating electrons, thereby shedding new light on the acceleration and transport of particles in other high-energy phenomena.

### [4] [The relationship between solar and stellar tachoclines, dynamos, and spin-down](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02211)
*Loren I. Matilsky,Lydia Korre,Nicholas Brummell*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This paper uses 3D nonlinear fluid simulations of a solar-like convective–radiative zone system to show that radiative spreading unconfined in a hydrodynamic case, but in a magnetohydrodynamic case with a seed magnetic field, a self-excited dynamo both confines the tachocline and extracts angular momentum from the radiative zone, linking tachocline dynamics to stellar spin-down and the global dynamo.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The solar tachocline's thinness is a dynamical mystery: radiative spreading should have caused it to thicken over the Sun's age, implying an unknown torque rigidifies the interior. Simultaneously, solar spin-down must transport angular momentum from the surface torques to the deep radiative zone while leaving the tachocline intact—two unresolved puzzles in solar/stellar dynamics.

Method: The authors perform two 3D, spherical-shell, fully nonlinear fluid simulations of a solar-like convective zone–radiative zone system: one hydrodynamic (HD) case without a magnetic field and one magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) case seeded with a small random magnetic field. They explicitly analyze the dynamics of radiative spreading in both configurations.

Result: Radiative spreading proceeds unmitigated in the HD simulation. In the MHD simulation, a self-excited dynamo both confines the tachocline to a thin layer and extracts angular momentum from the radiative zone, thereby communicating the spin-down torque downward.

Conclusion: Solar and stellar tachoclines may be intimately linked to both the spin-down process and the global dynamo—a unified magnetic mechanism may resolve the tachocline confinement and angular-momentum extraction puzzles simultaneously.

Abstract: The solar tachocline, a thin shear layer separating the differentially rotating convective zone (CZ) from the underlying rigidly rotating radiative zone (RZ), remains a dynamical mystery. Under the influence of "radiative spreading" (the process by which meridional circulation, baroclinicity, and differential rotation burrow through a stably stratified fluid), the tachocline should have spread significantly by the current age of the Sun. Some unknown torque must therefore rigidify the whole solar interior below the CZ and keep the tachocline confined to a thin layer. At the same time, the spin-down process (through which cool stars shed angular momentum via surface magnetic torques) must extract angular momentum from the solar RZ, presenting a second mystery: solar spin-down must be communicated from the near-surface layers to the deep interior, all the while leaving the tachocline intact. In this work, we explicitly analyze the dynamics of radiative spreading in two 3D, spherical-shell, fully nonlinear fluid simulations of a solar-like CZ--RZ system, one without a magnetic field (hydrodynamic---HD) and one with a small random seed magnetic field (magnetohydrodynamic---MHD). We find that radiative spreading is unmitigated in the HD case (as expected), but in the MHD case, a self-excited dynamo not only confines the tachocline, but \textit{also} extracts angular momentum from the RZ, thereby communicating the spin-down downward. We thus speculate that solar and stellar tachoclines may be intimately linked to both the spin-down process and global dynamo.

### [5] [Surface brightness inhomogeneity on the helium-rich hot subdwarf in the X-ray binary HD 49798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02314)

Frequency analysis identifies up to eleven frequencies forming an harmonic series with a 1.4 d period, matching the star's rotation period from spectroscopy. These brightenings are attributed to a slowly changing, inhomogeneous temperature distribution on the hot subdwarf's surface, possibly due to a magnetic field or Rossby waves.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To characterize the optical variability of the HD 49798 X-ray binary system and understand the nature of additional short-term brightness variations beyond the expected tidal deformation signal.

Method: Analysis of TESS optical light curves, including frequency analysis of the additional short-period variations and comparison of the detected harmonic series period with the independently derived stellar rotation period from spectroscopy.

Result: The light curve shows a sinusoidal variation from tidal deformation of the primary plus up to eleven harmonic frequencies based on a 1.4 d period, in excellent agreement with the spectroscopically inferred rotation period.

Conclusion: The brightenings originate from a slowly changing inhomogeneous surface temperature distribution on the hot subdwarf, potentially caused by a magnetic field or Rossby waves in the stellar envelope.

Abstract: HD 49798 is an X-ray binary consisting of a hot helium-rich subdwarf primary and an accreting compact companion with an orbital period of 1.55 d. TESS observations of its optical light curve suggest short-period brightenings superimposed on a sinusoidal variation. The latter can be attributed to the tidal deformation of the primary, while frequency analysis of the additional variations shows up to eleven frequencies which form an harmonic series based on a period of 1.4 d, in excellent agreement with the stellar rotation period inferred independently from spectroscopy. These brightenings may be explained by a slowly changing and inhomogeneous temperature distribution across the surface of the hot subdwarf, such as might be associated with a magnetic field or with the presence of Rossby waves in the stellar envelope.

### [8] [Solar Soft X-ray Coronal Dimming in a Failed Eruption Associated with Plasma Cooling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02555)
*Xinyue Wang,Astrid M. Veronig,Hechao Chen,Hui Tian*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: This study reports a peculiar deep coronal dimming event observed primarily in high-temperature emission (hot SXR and Fe XVIII/Fe XX lines) following a confined M8.8 solar flare with a failed filament eruption, showing no CME-driven mass loss. Plasma cooling from >5 MK temperatures is identified as the dominant mechanism, challenging the traditional assumption that deep hot-band dimmings always indicate CMEs.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Coronal dimmings in 1-2 MK lines are traditionally used as CME indicators. The authors aim to investigate whether deep high-temperature dimmings can occur without substantial CME-driven mass loss, which has implications for interpreting stellar dimmings as stellar CME signatures.

Method: The authors combined Sun-as-a-star observations (GOES/XRS and SDO/EVE) for soft X-ray and Fe XVIII/Fe XX intensity measurements, spatially resolved imaging (SDO/AIA) to localize the dimming region to the active region core, STEREO-A to rule out CME-driven mass loss, and differential emission measure (DEM) analysis to quantify plasma temperature evolution.

Result: SXR intensity reductions exceeded 30%, with measurable decreases in Fe XVIII (6.5 MK) and Fe XX (9.3 MK). The dimming originated from the active region core with no CME evidence from STEREO-A. DEM analysis showed plasma >5 MK cooling to lower temperatures, demonstrating plasma cooling dominates the hot-band dimming rather than mass escape.

Abstract: Coronal dimmings are observed as sudden and localized reductions in the extreme-ultraviolet and X-ray emission of the solar corona. Traditionally, significant dimmings of spectral lines formed at temperatures of 1-2 MK are regarded as indicators of coronal mass ejections (CMEs), reflecting the density depletion caused by plasma escaping into interplanetary space.
