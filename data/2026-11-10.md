### [1] [A panchromatic photometric and spectroscopic modeling of SN1987A from 200 to 800d](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10220)
*Luc Dessart*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: A grid of radiative-transfer models for Type II supernovae (Zsun/3, 11–21 Msun, E_kin=1.2e51 erg, ~0.08 Msun of 56Ni) is used to reproduce late-time (1–3 yr) SN1987A. [OI]6300A evolves non-monotonically with initial mass and is the only optical discriminant; the 13 Msun model best matches from UV to IR, with dust (~1e-4 to few×1e-3 Msun of 0.1 µm silicates) required beyond 500 d, and the IUE UV spectra found to be contaminated by a background hot star.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Late-time (1–3 yr) Type II SNe probe both the pre-supernova mass-loss history of massive stars and the chemistry of the inner, metal-rich (and potentially molecular/dusty) ejecta. Modeling SN1987A at these epochs constrains progenitor mass, 56Ni mass, ejecta energy, cooling by molecules, and the onset of dust formation.

Method: Grid of radiative-transfer models for Type II SNe at Z = Zsun/3, Minit = 11–21 Msun, E_kin = 1.2e51 erg fixed, M(56Ni) ~ 0.08 Msun. Analysis of the [OI]6300A flux evolution (1–2 yr) vs. Minit and metallicity, tests of CO (90% of C/O-shell cooling) and SiO cooling, comparison of synthetic spectra (UV to IR) with SN1987A, dust modeling with 0.1 µm silicate grains below ~2000 km/s, and re-examination of IUE 200–800 d spectra for background contamination.

Result: Metallicity effects at late times are weak. [OI]6300A at 1–2 yr rises with Minit (increasing O abundance) but drops for Minit > 17–19 Msun due to higher ejecta density, with little change elsewhere in the optical. CO cooling (90% of C/O-shell cooling) reduces [OI] by 10–20%, equivalent to an O(1 Msun) lower Minit for Minit < 19 Msun. Fractional [OI] flux rises from 200 to ~600 d, then declines. Good agreement with SN1987A for all Minit except [OI], the sole optical discriminant; the 13 Msun model best matches UV–IR, though higher Minit is possible with strong CO/SiO cooling. Beyond 500 d, dust below ~2000 km/s is needed to explain the ~1.5 mag excess fading vs. decay-power predictions; 0.1 µm silicates give Mdust ~ 1e-4 Msun at 600 d rising to a few×1e-3 Msun at 700–800 d. IUE 200–800 d spectra are contaminated by a background hot star, which also causes relative UV brightening past 500 d.

Conclusion: Late-time Type II SN spectra are governed mainly by ejecta density/O abundance rather than metallicity; [OI]6300A is the key mass discriminant, favoring ~13 Msun for SN1987A but allowing higher mass if strong CO/SiO cooling operates. Progressive dust condensation (up to a few×1e-3 Msun by 700–800 d) is essential to reproduce the optical fading, and background-source contamination must be accounted for when interpreting UV data of distant SNe II that fade to ~1e5 Lsun at >~2 yr.

Abstract: Type II supernovae (SNe) at 1-3yr after explosion can document not only the preSN mass loss history of massive stars but also the chemistry of the inner ejecta rich in metals and eventually molecules and dust. With the aim of modeling SN1987A, we present a grid of radiative-transfer models of Type II SNe at Zsun/3, covering an initial mass range between 11 and 21Msun, a fixed ejecta kinetic energy of 1.2e51erg, and ~0.08Msun of 56Ni. The influence of metallicity at such late times is found to be relatively weak. In this model set, the [OI]6330A flux at 1-2yr varies non-monotonically with Minit. It first rises with Minit due to the growing O abundance but drops for M>17-19Msun due to the higher ejecta density, with little change to the rest of the optical spectrum. Assuming CO molecules account for 90% of the cooling in the C/O shell leads to a 10-20% reduction in the [OI] flux, equivalent to an O(1Msun) reduction in Minit at M<19Msun. The fractional [OI] flux rises from 200 to ~600d but decreases beyond. We obtain a good agreement to SN1987A for all Minit apart from [OI], which appears in the optical as the only discriminant between models. The 13Msun model yields the best match, from UV to IR, but a higher Minit may result with allowance for strong CO and SiO cooling. Past 500d, a growing amount of dust below ~2000km/s is essential to account for the ~1.5mag optical excess fading relative to predictions from decay power. Adopting 0.1mic silicate grains, we find a dust mass of 1e-4Msun at 600d rising to a few 1e-3Msun at 700-800d. We also found that the 200-800d International Ultraviolet Explorer spectra of SN1987A are all contaminated by a background hot star, which also causes the relative UV brightening past 500d. Such contamination may complicate the interpretation of more distant SNe II at >~2yr once or if they fade to O(1e5Lsun) [abridged].

### [2] [Radial Velocity Confirmation of Two Binary Systems Detected with the Pulsation Timing Method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10959)
*Tomomi Otani,A. S. Baran,Kaydence Boyle,Julia M. Clark*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Follow-up spectroscopy confirms two TESS-identified subdwarf B+white dwarf binaries and yields refined, more precise radial-velocity orbits consistent with pulsation-timing predictions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: TIC 202354658 and TIC 69298924 were originally flagged as sdB+WD binaries via pulsation-timing and Fourier-modulation analyses of ultra-short-cadence TESS photometry; independent spectroscopic confirmation and more precise orbital parameters were needed.

Method: Radial velocity follow-up using Gemini-North and Nordic Optical Telescope spectra; RV curves derived and orbital solutions fitted for both targets, compared with pulsation-timing predictions.

Result: Orbital solutions agree with pulsation-timing predictions and are more precise than earlier results, confirming both objects as sdB+WD binaries. TIC 202354658 shows a relatively large eccentricity (e = 0.313 ± 0.013), uncommon for short-period sdB binaries.

Conclusion: Both targets are confirmed sdB+WD binary systems; the eccentric orbit of TIC 202354658 is noteworthy and warrants further study, and the agreement validates photometric timing/modulation techniques for binary identification.

Abstract: We present follow-up radial velocity (RV) results for the subdwarf B$+$white dwarf binaries TIC 202354658 and TIC 69298924, which were originally identified using the pulsation-timing and Fourier-modulation methods applied to ultra-short-cadence photometric data from the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite ($TESS$). Using spectra obtained with the Gemini-North telescope and the Nordic Optical Telescopes, we derived the radial velocity curves for both targets. The orbital solutions we obtained are consistent with the predictions based on pulsation timing, and they provide more precise orbital information compared with the previously obtained results. We therefore confirm that both TIC 202354658 and TIC 69298924 are binary systems with white dwarf companions. The RV solution of TIC 202354658 indicates a relatively large eccentricity ($e$ = 0.313 $\pm$ 0.013), which would be uncommon for a short orbital period sdB binary.

### [3] [The Role of Small-Scale Flux Emergence in Pre-eruptive Magnetic Destabilization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11096)
*Satoshi Inoue,Huu Minh Triet Nguyen*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Small-scale emerging flux (~0.6–3.0% of total flux) accelerates solar eruption onset by enhancing current-sheet thinning and reconnection in 3D MHD simulations.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: High-resolution observations show small-scale magnetic flux emerging just before flares, but its causal role in triggering eruptions is unclear. The study tests whether such emerging flux can destabilize a pre-eruptive configuration.

Method: Three-dimensional MHD simulations of a bipolar active region built by potential-field extrapolation from a sunspot pair. Sustained rotational motions build strong shear along the polarity inversion line. A small-scale emerging flux (0.6%–3.0% of total flux) with polarity opposite to the main sunspot is introduced at the bipole center, and cases with and without it are compared.

Result: Pre-existing field shows no significant early-phase differences with or without the emerging flux. However, the current sheet in the sheared field lines thins/collapses more rapidly, and reconnection-driven eruption starts earlier when small-scale emerging flux is present. Involvement of highly twisted field lines during the pre-eruption phase critically sets the eruption onset time.

Conclusion: Small-scale emerging flux accelerates destabilization of the pre-eruptive configuration via enhanced current-sheet thinning and magnetic reconnection, leading to earlier eruption onset; highly twisted field lines are decisive for onset timing.

Abstract: Recent high-resolution observations have reported the emergence of small-scale magnetic flux shortly before the onset of solar flares; however, their role in solar eruption processes remains unclear. In this study, we perform three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations to examine the effect of small-scale emerging flux on the eruption process. We construct a bipolar active region by extrapolating a potential magnetic field from a pair of sunspots and drive the system with sustained rotational motions to build up strong shear along the polarity inversion line. A small-scale emerging flux, amounting to approximately 0.6%-3.0% of the total magnetic flux and having a polarity opposite to that of the main sunspot, is then introduced at the center of the bipole. As a result, although no significant differences are found in the pre-existing magnetic field during the early phase with or without the emerging flux, the current sheet formed in the sheared field lines collapses more rapidly, and the reconnection-driven eruption is initiated earlier in the case with small-scale emerging flux. This result suggests that small-scale emerging flux accelerates the destabilization of the pre-eruptive magnetic configuration through enhanced current-sheet thinning and magnetic reconnection, leading to an earlier eruption onset. Furthermore, we find that the involvement of highly twisted field lines during the pre-eruption phase plays a crucial role in determining the eruption onset time.

### [4] [Solar cycle variations of meridional flow and differential rotation in a non-kinematic dynamo model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11548)
*Bidya Binay Karak,Mark Miesch*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: The authors develop a non-kinematic solar dynamo model in which large-scale flows are self-consistently generated rather than prescribed, and show it reproduces solar-cycle variations in meridional flow and differential rotation, including torsional oscillations.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Flux transport dynamo models dominate explanations of the solar cycle, but most are kinematic and axisymmetric, prescribing the large-scale flows rather than deriving them. This limits physical realism and prevents capturing feedback between magnetic fields, flows, and thermodynamics.

Method: A non-kinematic dynamo model is constructed by specifying the large-scale flows (differential rotation, meridional flow) so that no Λ-effect or turbulent-transport parameterisation is needed to drive them; only fluctuating flows and thermodynamics are solved. The model is inherently 3D but currently run in axisymmetric mode because the Babcock-Leighton α source is taken as axisymmetric.

Result: The model reproduces solar-cycle variations in both meridional flow and differential rotation, exhibiting torsional oscillations in some agreement with observations, and shows natural cycle-to-cycle variability without imposed stochastic fluctuations.

Conclusion: The work offers a new methodology for modelling the non-kinematic solar dynamo in 3D, capturing flow-field feedback and intrinsic variability.

Abstract: Flux transport dynamo models have emerged as the leading paradigm for explaining the observed solar cycle. However, most of these models are kinematic and axisymmetric. We develop a non-kinematic dynamo model by specifying large-scale flows, such as the differential rotation and meridional flow. Thus, our model does not require the $Λ$ effect or other parameterisations of turbulent transport to generate the large-scale flows; it only solves the fluctuating flows and thermodynamics. Our model is inherently 3D by construction, although it is currently operating in axisymmetric mode because the source for poloidal field generation (Babcock-Leighton $α$) is assumed to be axisymmetric. Our model reproduces solar-cycle variations in both the meridional flow and the differential rotation -- specifically, the torsional oscillation in some agreement with observations. The model naturally exhibits some variability in the solar cycle, even without incorporating stochastic fluctuations, offering a new methodology for modelling the non-kinematic solar dynamo in 3D.

### [5] [A Census of Novae in the Small Magellanic Cloud, 1897-2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11664)
*Allen W. Shafter*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: A census identifies 26 optical transients reported as novae/nova-like eruptions in the Small Magellanic Cloud from 1897 (HV 849) through October 2026, of which 23 are probable classical novae and several are fast super-Eddington Be+white dwarf eruptions.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The paper aims to compile and characterize the population of nova candidates in the SMC to establish a complete census of reported eruptions over more than a century.

Method: A literature/archival compilation of reported optical transients classified as novae or nova-like, cross-matched and categorized by eruption type, including identification of quiescent counterparts and X-ray-detected candidates.

Result: 26 optical transients are tabulated; 23 are probable classical novae. MAXI J0158−744 and CXOU J005245.0−722844 are very fast, super-Eddington eruptions interpreted as novae on white dwarfs with Be-star companions; SMCN 2002-10a is a possible Be+WD event found via its quiescent counterpart. Four additional X-ray-only Be+WD candidates are briefly discussed.

Conclusion: The SMC nova census comprises predominantly classical novae, with a distinct subpopulation of fast super-Eddington Be+WD systems, indicating that X-ray surveys are uncovering a hidden Be+WD nova class beyond optical discoveries.

Abstract: A census of nova candidates in the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) is presented. Twenty-six optical transients have been reported as novae or nova-like eruptions from the discovery of HV 849 in 1897 through 2026 October. Twenty-three are probable classical novae. Two, MAXI J0158$-$744 and CXOU J005245.0$-$722844, are very fast, super-Eddington eruptions that have been interpreted as novae on white dwarfs with Be-star companions, while a third, SMCN 2002-10a, is a possible Be+WD event identified only from its quiescent counterpart. Four additional Be+WD candidates that have been detected only in X-rays are briefly discussed.

### [6] [Enhanced Mid-Photospheric Heating Around a Solar Pore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12121)
*Ana Belén Griñón-Marín,Adur Pastor Yabar,Jaime de la Cruz Rodríguez,Sami K. Solanki,Andreas Lagg,Achim Gandorfer,Jose Carlos del Toro Iniesta,Yukio Katsukawa,Pietro Bernasconi,Thomas Berkefeld,Alex Feller,Tino L. Riethmüller,Alberto Álvarez-Herrero,Masahito Kubo,H. N. Smitha,David Orozco Suárez,Bianca Grauf,Michael Carpenter,Alexander Bell,Valentín Martínez Pillet,Francisco Javier Bailén,Julian Blanco Rodríguez,Juan Sebastián Castellanos Durán,Edvarda Harnes,Johannes Hoelken,Francisco A. Iglesias,Ryohtaroh T. Ishikawa,Yusuke Kawabata,Takuma Matsumoto,Takayoshi Oba,Azaymi L. Siu-Tapia,Hanna Strecker,Dušan Vukadinović,Hirohisa Hara,Toshifumi Shimizu*

Main category: astro-ph.SR

TL;DR: Depth-stratified NLTE spectropolarimetric inversions of a solar pore with Sunrise III/SCIP reveal transonic boundary downflows, a shrinking thermal contrast with height, and patchy ~300 K mid-photospheric heating co-spatial with an expanding magnetic canopy.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Pores are relatively simple magnetic structures that can serve as laboratories for studying the coupling between magnetic topology, convection, and atmospheric energy transport from the photosphere to the chromosphere; such depth-resolved, temporally extended characterizations are still scarce.

Method: Spatially coupled NLTE spectropolarimetric inversions of a solar pore observed with the SCIP instrument aboard Sunrise III, using the Ca II 8542 Å line (among others), over roughly two hours and three rasters, to retrieve the thermal, magnetic, and velocity stratification from the photosphere to the chromosphere.

Result: Inversions recover a canonical cool umbra with strong, mostly vertical fields. Most photospheric velocities are subsonic, but localized downflows near the pore boundary reach transonic or potentially supersonic speeds around log τ500 = 0. Thermal contrast between the pore and its surroundings decreases substantially at higher layers. Localized mid-photospheric temperature enhancements (~300 K, excess thermal-energy density up to 170 J m^-3) form a patchy, ring-like structure in the Ca II 8542 Å wings without a clear continuum counterpart; these regions coincide with enhanced transverse field and the expanding magnetic canopy and appear in all three rasters, though the cadence cannot determine whether individual patches persist or re-form.

Conclusion: The heating mechanism cannot be uniquely identified, but the inferred magnetic topology supports an association with processes in the expanding magnetic canopy. Pores are valuable for investigating the coupling between magnetic topology, convection, and atmospheric energy transport.

Abstract: We present depth-stratified atmospheric reconstructions of a solar pore observed with the Sunrise Chromospheric Infrared SpectroPolarimeter (SCIP) aboard \textsc{Sunrise~iii}. Using spatially coupled NLTE spectropolarimetric inversions, we characterize the thermal, magnetic, and velocity structure of the pore and its surroundings from the photosphere to the chromosphere over approximately two hours. The inversions recover the canonical photospheric properties of pores: a cool umbra with strong magnetic fields dominated by the vertical component. Most photospheric velocities are subsonic, but localized downflows near the pore boundary reach transonic or potentially supersonic values around $\logτ_{500}=0$. At higher atmospheric layers, the thermal contrast between the pore and its surroundings decreases substantially. We also detect localized mid-photospheric temperature enhancements forming a patchy, ring-like structure in the wings of the Ca\,{\sc ii} 8542\,Å line, without a clear continuum counterpart. The temperature excess reaches approximately $300$\,K and corresponds to an excess thermal-energy density of up to $170$\,J\,m$^{-3}$. These regions coincide with an enhanced transverse magnetic-field component and the expanding magnetic canopy surrounding the pore. The structure is detected in all three rasters, although the cadence does not establish whether individual patches persist or repeatedly re-form. While the heating mechanism cannot be uniquely identified, the inferred magnetic topology supports an association with processes operating in the expanding canopy. These results demonstrate the value of pores for investigating the coupling between magnetic topology, convection, and atmospheric energy transport.
