Main category: astro-ph.SR

  <summary>Details</summary>

Abstract: Observations of hot coronal loops have often shown the presence of compressive plasma oscillations. These oscillations are usually preceded by a flare-associated localised brightening in the vicinity of one of the footpoints of the coronal loop. The flare heating produces an impulsive injection of plasma into the coronal loop, along with a plasma perturbation that bounces back and forth between the two footpoints, termed as sloshing oscillations. While the association of these oscillations with flares is apparent, the pertinent connection between their excitation and the flare characteristics remains poorly understood. In this study, using data from the Atmospheric Imaging Assembly (AIA) onboard the Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO), we present a unique set of observations, where the response of a single coronal loop due to repeated flares in the same active region is evident. Our analysis reveals that in three out of five flare events, sloshing oscillations are triggered, while in the other two events, only plasma flows are observed. By examining the duration of plasma injection associated with each flare, we find that sloshing oscillations occur when the injection duration is shorter than the travel time of the plasma perturbation across the loop. In contrast, no oscillations are observed when the injection duration is comparable to the travel time. These results reveal the dependence of sloshing oscillation excitation on the temporal characteristics of flares.
